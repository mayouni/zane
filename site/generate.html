How do I hold an AI generator to the law?

Two layers: a grammar that fixes the shape of what a generator can write, and a court that refuses what the law forbids. They hold different things, and this page says which holds what.

A declaration the grammar allows and the law refuses

An AI asked to build a business interface reproduces the habits it was trained on, politely and at machine speed. Make it declare intent first, and the intent can be checked before anything is drawn. Here is a declaration in which every clause is one the language has; the law still refuses the flow:

// what a generator may well emit: every clause is allowed
DEFINE INTENT_FLOW pick_and_pay (
  SATISFIES [SELECT, ACT],
  ALWAYS_REACHABLE [UNDO, CANCEL, EXIT],
  ACTOR operator
)
$ node tools/zui/cli.js check pick.zui
pick.zui:2  error  SELECT_ACT_COLLAPSE  pick_and_pay
    Selection has become execution: SATISFIES holds SELECT and ACT without CONFIRM.
    Rule 16 · Intentional Clicks · Article II

1 error(s), 0 warning(s) across 1 file(s) at level 2

That is a real run, re-run by this site's own check. The refusal names the rule it rests on, which is what lets a generator be told which law it broke instead of being told it was wrong.

The grammar holds the shape

The language is declared in Haro, and the forge writes from that declaration a grammar a decoder can be held to. It is checked in as haro/zui.gbnf, a few kilobytes of GBNF, the notation grammar-constrained decoders accept.

What it fixes: the 10 kinds of declaration, which clauses each kind may carry, and the lexical form (comments, quoted strings, upper-case keywords, lower-case names, trailing commas). A clause that does not belong to a kind cannot be written under it: a role on a screen, for example, is not a sentence of this grammar.

What it does not hold: the verbs. A list of names is any identifier, so an unknown verb is a sentence the grammar accepts and the court refuses. Neither does it hold anything that needs two clauses read together: the declaration above is well-formed. Every refusal of meaning stays the court's. The grammar narrows what a generator can say; it does not make it lawful.

How far it is checked: the judge regenerates the file from the declaration on every run and fails if the copy checked in differs, so the file cannot drift from the forge. It has not been run under a decoder in this repository: no model has been decoded under it here, and the site does not claim one has.

A loop you can run today

With any capable assistant: put the law in its context, instruct it to declare each surface in .zui before rendering it, run the verifier, and hand it the findings by rule number:

You are building under the Zui Constitution (rules.json attached).
Declare each surface in .zui before rendering it, and cite the rules you obeyed.
Here is the verifier's report on your last declaration. Fix every finding, by rule:
pick.zui:2 error SELECT_ACT_COLLAPSE (Rule 16)

This is a procedure, not a measured result: nobody has yet measured how many rounds a model needs under it, or whether the declared surface and the rendered one stay in step. The runtime half is the audit, which measures what was actually painted (the UI audit).

Feel what the law forbids first

The defects a generator reproduces have rules behind them. What a violation feels like lets you commit each family of offence yourself, safely fenced, each beside its lawful twin, before you ask a model to avoid them.

What is not written

The law already says how an agent may act on an interface: it is a named actor under the same audit as a person, its output is a reviewable proposal, and what it produces is marked as machine-made. What is not written is the jurisdiction over the chat stream, and a surface that serves the law to an agent while it works. That is unchanged and held, and nothing on this site advances it.
