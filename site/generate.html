Use · generate · you build interfaces with AI

The generate workflow holds an AI generator to the law with a grammar that fixes what it can write and a court that refuses what the law forbids.

The generate workflow holds an AI generator to the law through two layers: a grammar that fixes the shape of what the generator can write, and a court that refuses what the law forbids. The workflow lets a team check what a generator intends before anything is drawn, and tells the generator which rule it broke. The two layers hold different things, and this page says which layer holds what.

A declaration the grammar allows and the law refuses

An AI that is asked to build a business interface reproduces the habits it was trained on, politely and at machine speed. When a generator must declare intent first, the intent can be checked before anything is drawn. The declaration below uses only clauses that the language has, and the law still refuses the flow:

// what a generator may well emit: every clause is allowed
DEFINE INTENT_FLOW pick_and_pay (
  SATISFIES [SELECT, ACT],
  ALWAYS_REACHABLE [UNDO, CANCEL, EXIT],
  ACTOR operator
)
$ node tools/zane/cli.js check pick.zane
pick.zane:2  error  SELECT_ACT_COLLAPSE  pick_and_pay
    Selection has become execution: SATISFIES holds SELECT and ACT without CONFIRM.
    Rule 16 · Intentional Clicks · Article II

1 error(s), 0 warning(s) across 1 file(s) at level 2

The run above is real, and this site's own check re-runs it. The refusal names the rule it rests on, and this lets a generator be told which law it broke instead of being told only that it was wrong.

What the grammar fixes, what it does not hold, and how far it is checked

The language is declared in Haro, and from that declaration the forge writes a grammar that a decoder can be held to. The grammar is checked in as haro/zane.gbnf, a few kilobytes of GBNF, which is the notation that grammar-constrained decoders accept.

The grammar fixes the 10 kinds of declaration, the clauses each kind may carry, and the lexical form (comments, quoted strings, upper-case keywords, lower-case names, trailing commas). A clause that does not belong to a kind cannot be written under it: a role on a screen, for example, is not a sentence of this grammar.

The grammar does not hold the verbs. A list of names accepts any identifier, so an unknown verb is a sentence that the grammar accepts and the court refuses. The grammar also does not hold anything that needs two clauses read together: the declaration above is well-formed. Every refusal of meaning stays with the court. The grammar narrows what a generator can say, and it does not make what the generator says lawful.

The grammar is checked against its source: the judge regenerates the file from the declaration on every run and fails if the checked-in copy differs, so the file cannot drift from the forge. The grammar has not been run under a decoder in this repository: no model has been decoded under it here, and the site does not claim that one has.

A loop with an assistant that you can run today

With any capable assistant, you put the law in its context, instruct it to declare each surface in .zane before rendering it, run the verifier, and hand it the findings by rule number:

You are building under the Zane Constitution (rules.json attached).
Declare each surface in .zane before rendering it, and cite the rules you obeyed.
Here is the verifier's report on your last declaration. Fix every finding, by rule:
pick.zane:2 error SELECT_ACT_COLLAPSE (Rule 16)

This loop is a procedure, not a measured result: nobody has yet measured how many rounds a model needs under it, or whether the declared surface and the rendered surface stay in step. The runtime half is the UI audit, which measures what was actually painted.

Pages that let you commit each violation before asking a generator to avoid it

The defects that a generator reproduces have rules behind them. What a violation feels like lets you commit each family of offence yourself, safely fenced and beside its lawful twin, before you ask a model to avoid them.

The part of the law for agents that is not written yet

The law already says how an agent may act on an interface: the agent is a named actor under the same audit as a person, its output is a reviewable proposal, and what it produces is marked as machine-made. Two things are not written: the jurisdiction over the chat stream, and a surface that serves the law to an agent while it works. Both are unchanged and held, and nothing on this site advances them.
