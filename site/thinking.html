Why · for a designer who wants the reasoning

The reasoning behind Zayn's approach is that an interface declares what a user, a person or an agent, is doing, and that every rendering is a consequence of that declaration.

This reasoning explains why Zayn's approach starts an interface from a declaration of what the user is doing: so that one law can be applied to that declaration in every channel. The approach lets a tool check whether a user can leave, undo and see where they are before anything has been drawn. This page explains the approach in eight parts: what it inverts, why the vocabulary is closed, what is unified and what stays different, and which questions are still open. Each part names the design document it comes from, and says when that document is a proposal and not a ruling.

Why Zayn declares intent first and treats rendering as a consequence

Most UI frameworks begin from a physical assumption, such as screens, widgets and layouts, and try to infer what the user intends. Zayn inverts this order. A user comes to an interface to discover, focus, read, compare, select, act, confirm and understand what followed, and not to click a button. Zayn declares what the user is doing, and the rendering follows from that declaration. From the founding architecture, section 1.

This inversion makes correctness checkable. When the unit of declaration is a widget, a tool can check only the widget. When the unit of declaration is what the user is doing, a tool can check whether the user can leave, undo and see where they are, before anything has been drawn. The architecture has three layers in a strict order, and each lower layer constrains the layers above it:

Why the grammar has a closed set of verbs, shared among six activities

The grammar has 22 verbs, and the set is closed on purpose: a new channel adds renderings and never adds verbs. Closure keeps the map of what a user can do in each channel finite, so that a gap is a count and not an opinion. From the founding architecture, section 2.

The verbs are shared out once each among six activities. Each activity is a question a user asks, in the order a user meets an interface: perceive (whether something is there, and in what state), understand, decide, act, recover and trust.

Two of the six activities, perceive and trust, own no verb. A user senses a state before using any verb, and trust is a property of every verb rather than a verb of its own.

This choice keeps the map true of a lamp, which has no READ verb and whose state a person still perceives across a room. The grid page draws the six activities against the ten channels and shows where the law has nothing to say.

The rights the law guarantees, and the articles each rule derives from

Every rule in the constitution states why it exists, because a rule without a reason is a matter of taste. Each rule derives from one of 7 articles (Calm, Intent Over Mechanics, Visible State, Reversibility, Predictable Authority, Operator Primacy and Vibes, and Amendment), and the law guarantees 6 rights to every person who uses the interface: the rights to understand, to reverse, to know, to leave, to act without fear, and to reach a human.

A refusal cites the rule, the article and the right it rests on, so a review of the refusals is an audit. The rules page shows one rule as the tools load it, who can catch a violation of it today, and how the law changes.

The law is deliberately narrow: it serves business applications first, for people who use the same screens for hours under pressure. Its stance is stated in four principles: Calm over power, intent over mechanics, visible over hidden, undo is dignity. A rule that needs an argument from consumer delight is outside the law's jurisdiction.

What is unified across channels and what stays different in each channel

The design rests on one sentence with three parts. One declaration states what the user is doing and what things mean, independent of channel, culture and technology. Many renderings carry that declaration on a screen, in sound, by voice, on a lamp, on a page, on a sign or through an actuator, and each rendering is genuinely different and does not pretend otherwise. One law states the invariant that every rendering must keep, and its court refuses a rendering that breaks it. From the interaction model, section 3.

The model states which parts are unified and which parts must stay separate. The intent, the meaning, the law, the refusal envelope and the question what does this mean, and to whom are unified.

The way each channel serves an intent, the colour, timbre or word that carries a meaning, the instrument that can enforce each rule, each court's own codes and each device's transducers stay separate. A model that unifies too much misrepresents the channels, and a model that unifies too little is a pile of unrelated parts.

The boundary between the two is declared: SURFACE_AS names the channel, the matrix states what the channel can carry, and the culture axis names the culture.

The same three-part shape appears independently in three unrelated parts of the project, for unrelated reasons. This independence is the evidence that the shape is a property of the system and not an opinion of its author. From the interaction model, sections 2 and 5.

The sentence alone proves nothing. The Channels page shows the instrument that tests it and reports both of its results: the sentence is proved for screen against sound.

How sensors and actuators fit the closed vocabulary without new verbs

When a building, a vehicle or a greenhouse is the interface, the obvious move is to add sensors and actuators to the list of channels. That move would corrupt the channel axis, because a channel answers one question: through what does a meaning reach a person?

A thermometer renders nothing, and a motor renders nothing; they are the system's other two ports. A system has three ports: render, to a person's senses; sense, from a person or the world; and actuate, onto the world.

Channels classify only the render port. Sensors and actuators reach a person only when something else renders them: a motor turning is not a rendering, and a person hearing the motor turn is one. From the sensors and actuators study, section 1.

The study reports where the physical world puts pressure on the design. The 22 verbs hold: opening a valve renders ACT onto the world, as a button renders ACT onto a screen, so no verb is added. The strata absorb the other cases.

The law is the part that bends: four rules meet cases beyond the ones they were written for. Undo meets a world that does not undo; perceivable state meets a system that knows too much; the hundred milliseconds meet physical time; and the law meets a bystander who never chose the interface.

The study states plainly that nothing structural breaks, and that this is a better result than the framework deserved. From the study, sections 5 and 6.

This section is design reasoning, and the study it draws on names its four collisions between culture and channel.

How culture is proposed as a third axis, and what Article VI already allows a culture to change

A statement of what an expression does, and of the channel that carries it, still does not say what the expression means to the person who receives it. A person reads a colour, a flag used for a language, or a gesture through a culture.

The wider design proposes culture as a third axis beside the intent and the channel, with a boundary between what the law fixes everywhere and what a culture may vary. That design, the Second Founding, is a proposal, and this site rests on the ratified law. The document describes it.

Article VI, operator primacy and vibes, already exists: an interaction culture may set density, motion and surface, and may never remove an intent that another declaration guarantees. This site has two cultures, Modern and Retro, and the select at the foot of every page switches between them. The two-cultures exhibit renders one shared truth in both cultures at once, and shows that the rights are the same in both.

Why style is treated as data emitted from meaning, rather than written by hand

Applied to appearance, the same logic gives an unexpected verdict. A style sheet is not a list of looks: it is the resolution from meaning to rendering, and the study makes that resolution declarable, versioned and checkable.

Every hand-written style rule is a meaning that escapes the semantic layer, in the same way as a hex colour written in code: the court cannot see it, and the culture axis cannot vary it.

The study's doctrine is therefore that style is emitted, never authored. Where a project writes style by hand, the study states that the court is blind there, and does not state that hand-written style is forbidden. From stzSense, sections 1 and 12.

This is a design verdict: the study proposes the format and its first coverage.

The measured seams of the approach, and the questions that are still open

The approach has open ends, and they are measured. The language is declared a second time as data, and a court is forged from that declaration. That court and the hand-written verifier disagree on 22 of the suite's 77 fixtures. Each disagreement belongs to a named seam (9 seams in all), and a ratchet counts and holds them. The Declared in Haro page lists them.

The interaction model lists, in its own words, four things that are missing before the model is whole. First, the declaration is spread across four artifacts that do not reference each other. Second, judgment is made per language and not per system, so parts that are each lawful can be unlawful in combination.

Third, context (which channels, which culture, which device) is not declared, so it cannot be checked. Fourth, the channels must agree with each other, and the instrument on the Channels page measures that agreement against the data. From the interaction model, section 6.

The status board states, part by part, what exists, what is designed and what is held: the board.

The four names used on this site, and what each one names

Four names appear on this site, and this section defines each one so that a stranger does not have to guess. Zayn is the language and the project: declarations of what a user is doing, with their law, their verifier and their tools. Zayn was called Zane until November 2026, and Zui until 5 November 2026. Inside the Softanza family its formal name is StzZui.

Haro is a language for declaring languages, and Zayn is declared a second time in Haro. Softanza is the family of projects that all of this belongs to.

The reasoning on this page comes from these documents, in this order: the founding architecture, the interaction model, sensors and actuators, stzSense, a GUI system, read by the law, the coverage map, and the Second Founding, a proposal.
