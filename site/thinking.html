Approach · for a designer who wants the reasoning

An interface is what a person is doing, rendered. Everything else is a consequence.

An essay in eight parts on why Zane starts from intent: what it inverts, why the vocabulary is closed, what is unified and what must stay different, and where the thinking is still open. Each part names the design document it comes from, and says when that document is a proposal and not a ruling.

The inversion: intent first

Most UI frameworks begin from a physical assumption, screens and widgets and layouts, and try to infer what the person intends. Zane inverts the order. A person does not come to an interface to click a button; they come to discover, focus, read, compare, select, act, confirm and understand what followed. Rendering is a consequence of that, and not the thing declared. From the founding architecture, section 1.

That inversion is what makes correctness checkable. If the unit of declaration is a widget, the only thing a tool can check is the widget. If it is what the person is doing, a tool can ask whether they can leave, undo, and see where they are, before anything has been drawn. Why a closed set of verbs, in six activities

The grammar has 22 verbs, and it is closed on purpose: a new channel adds renderings, never verbs. Closure is what lets a map of what a person can do in each channel be finite, so that a gap can be a count and not an opinion. From the founding architecture, section 2.

The verbs are shared out, once each, among six activities that are the questions a person is asking in the order they meet an interface: perceive (is there something, and in what state), understand, decide, act, recover and trust.

Two of the six, perceive and trust, own no verb, because a state is sensed before any verb is used and trust is a property of every verb and not one of them.

That choice is what keeps the map true of a lamp, which has no READ and whose state is still perceived across a room. The grid draws the six against the ten channels, and says where the law has nothing to say.

Rights, and the articles they come from

A rule that cannot say why it exists is a taste. In the constitution every rule derives from one of 7 articles (Calm, Intent Over Mechanics, Visible State, Reversibility, Predictable Authority, Operator Primacy and Vibes, and Amendment), and the law guarantees 6 rights to the person using the interface: to understand, to reverse, to know, to leave, to act without fear, and to a human.

A refusal cites the rule, the article and the right it rests on, so a review is an audit. The rules page shows one rule as the tools load it, who can catch a violation of it today, and how the law changes.

The stance behind it is openly narrow: business applications first, for people who use the same screens for hours under pressure. Calm over power, intent over mechanics, visible over hidden, undo is dignity. Where a rule needs a consumer-delight argument, it is out of jurisdiction.

One declaration, many renderings, one law

The sentence the design rests on has three parts. One declaration: what the person is doing and what things mean, blind to channel, culture and technology. Many renderings: screen, sound, voice, lamp, page, sign, actuator, each genuinely different, and never pretending otherwise. One law: the invariant that must survive every rendering, and the court that refuses when it does not. From the interaction model, section 3.

The discipline is in knowing what is unified and what must not be. The intent, the meaning, the law, the refusal envelope and the question what does this mean, and to whom are one.

How each channel serves an intent, what colour or timbre or word carries a meaning, which instrument can enforce which rule, each court's own codes and every device's transducers are many. Over-unify and the model lies; under-unify and it is a pile of parts.

And the seam is declared and not hoped for: the channel is named by SURFACE_AS, what it can carry by the matrix, and the culture by its own axis.

The same shape was found independently in three unrelated parts of the project, for unrelated reasons, which is the evidence that it is a property of the system and not an opinion of its author. From the interaction model, sections 2 and 5.

A sentence like that is cheap. The Channels page shows the instrument that tests it, and what it says: proved for one pair of channels, and not proved for another, which is where it is honest.

Senses and actuators: what the closed vocabulary survives

The obvious move, when a building, a vehicle or a greenhouse is the interface, is to add sensors and actuators to the list of channels. That would corrupt the axis. A channel answers one question: through what does a meaning reach a person?

A thermometer renders nothing and a motor renders nothing; they are the system's other two ports. A system has three ports, not one: render (to a person's senses), sense (from a person or the world), and actuate (onto the world).

Channels classify only the first, and sensors and actuators reach a person only when something else renders them: a motor turning is not a rendering, a person hearing it is. From the sensors and actuators study, section 1.

The result worth reporting is where the pressure landed. The 22 verbs survived: opening a valve is a rendering of ACT into the world, as a button is a rendering of it onto a screen, so no verb was added. The strata absorbed the rest.

What bent was the law: four rules meet cases they were not written for (undo meeting a world that does not undo; perceivable state meeting a system that knows too much; the hundred milliseconds meeting physical time; the bystander who never chose the interface).

The document states plainly that nothing structural broke and that this is a better result than the framework deserved. From the study, sections 5 and 6.

Limit: this is design reasoning. The study's four collisions are named and not yet resolved into rules, and the rules for the physical channels are held until a product that ships in one is named.

Culture: a third axis, and a switch that is not a skin

A statement of what an expression does, and of where it is carried, still does not say what it means to the person receiving it. Colour, a flag used for a language, a gesture: meaning is read through a culture.

The wider design proposes culture as a third axis beside the intent and the channel, with a boundary between what the law fixes everywhere and what a culture may vary. That design, the Second Founding, is proposed and not ratified, and nothing on this site rests on it. The document is the place to read it.

What does exist is Article VI, operator primacy and vibes: an interaction culture may set density, motion and surface, and may never remove an intent another declaration guarantees. This site has two, Modern and Retro, and the select at the foot of every page is the control. The two-cultures exhibit renders one shared truth in both at once, to show that the rights do not flex.

Style is the resolution from meaning to rendering

The same logic applied to appearance gives an unexpected verdict. A style sheet is not a list of looks; it is the resolution from meaning to rendering, made declarable, versioned and checkable.

Every hand-written style rule is a meaning that escaped the semantic layer, exactly as a hex colour in code is: the court cannot see it and the culture axis cannot vary it.

So the doctrine in the study is that style is emitted, never authored, and where a project writes it by hand the honest statement is that the court is blind there, not that it is forbidden. From stzSense, sections 1 and 12.

Limit: a design verdict. The study proposes the format and its first small coverage; the site does not claim it is built.

The seams, and what is still open

The thinking has loose ends, and they are measured. The language is declared a second time as data and a court is forged from it; that court and the hand-written verifier disagree on 18 of the suite's 73 fixtures, and each is a named seam (8 of them), counted and held by a ratchet. Declared in Haro has them.

The interaction model lists four things missing before it is whole, and says so in its own words. The declaration is spread across four artifacts that do not reference each other. Judgment is per language and not per system, so parts that are each lawful can be unlawful between them.

Context (which channels, which culture, which device) is not declared and so cannot be checked. And nothing proved cross-channel agreement, which the instrument on the Channels page now begins to do, and does not yet complete. From the interaction model, section 6.

And the status board says, part by part, what exists, what is designed and what is held: the board.

Who is who

Five names appear on this site, and a stranger should not have to guess. Zane is the language, and the project: declarations of what a person is doing, with their law, their verifier and their tools. It was called Zui until 5 November 2026. Inside the Softanza family its formal name is StzZui.

Zing is a separate open-source framework and the first tool that consumes the law: its zing check reads the same file and cites the same rules. Haro is a language for declaring languages, in which Zane is declared a second time. Softanza is the family of projects all of this belongs to.

The reasoning above comes from these documents, in this order: the founding architecture, the interaction model, sensors and actuators, stzSense, a GUI system, read by the law, the coverage map, and, proposed and not ratified, the Second Founding.
