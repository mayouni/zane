The grid

Each row is a channel (what each is made of is on the Channels page), each column is something a person does. A cell shows how many rules are written for that channel and that activity, and how many of the suite's 73 fixtures sit there. Closed means the channel cannot carry any verb the activity owns, so the person cannot do it there at all; no rule is meant to sit on a closed cell. None is the finding: the cell is open and no rule is written for it.

The rows of a person's day

Six activities. Each is a question the person is asking, in the order they meet an interface, and none is the name of a defect:

ActivityThe questionVerbs it owns
perceiveIs there something, and what state is it in?none
understandWhat is this, where am I, what does it say?DISCOVER UNDERSTAND LOCATE READ SCAN SCROLL ZOOM COMPARE
decideWhat can I choose, and can I narrow it?FOCUS FILTER SELECT HIGHLIGHT PREVIEW MARK
actCan I do the thing, and know that it was done?ACT CONFIRM
recoverCan I go back, stop, try again, leave?CANCEL UNDO PAUSE RESUME RETRY EXIT
trustCan I rely on who or what is acting, and on what it tells me?none

The 22 verbs of the intent grammar are shared out once each, so the rows are the language's own verbs grouped by what the person is doing, and not a classification invented beside it. Perceive and trust own no verb on purpose: a state is sensed before any verb is used, and trust is a property of every verb. That is also what keeps the map true of a lamp, which has no READ and whose state is still perceived across a room.

The closed cells

A cell is closed when its activity owns verbs and the channel marks every one of them absent. 11 of the cells are closed, derived from media.json the way the verifier derives what a channel cannot carry: understand on haptic, decide on haptic, print, luminous and signage, act on print, luminous and signage, and recover on print, luminous and signage. They are not counted as gaps. The Channels page shows what each channel is made of, and why.

The gap, as a finding

  • The law is written for a screen. 94 of the 121 placed rules are written for the screen column, and 89 of them for nothing else. 26 are stated without a channel, and 6 name another.
  • 5 of the ten channels have no rule written for them: haptic, luminous, signage, spatial and terminal. A rule reaches them only if it is stated without a channel, which says it can be projected there and not that anyone has done it. Counting that reach as coverage would make the gap read zero, and nobody checked the projection once, so it is not counted.
  • A rule on a cell is not a cell covered. 80 of the 123 rules need a person's judgment. A rule only a person can apply has been written, and says nothing about whether anything checks it.
  • Rules for the five uncovered channels are held, not advanced, until a product that ships in one is named. The grid says where the law is silent; it proposes no rule.

Limit: nobody has heard, felt or looked at a channel to check a cell. The grid is a document about perception, so this phase is reported as designed and measured, and not as verified by a person.

How far the placement can be trusted

The placement of the rules on the grid is one reader's first cut: each rule's activities were read from its title and reason, and its channels from its wording. Another reader would move some, and the counts move with them. So it was measured, not changed: two readers who had seen none of it re-placed samples, and the figures below are how many placements differed. They are the figures as measured on 3 November 2026, against the placement as it stood then; a ruling since has moved four rules, so running the comparison scripts today gives other figures, and the saved outputs are the record.

The readWhat it re-placedActivities and channel both agree
First, a sample taken by positionevery fourth placed rule, by number6 of 31
Second, exactly the rules that decide the empty cellsevery rule stated without a channel, and every rule named for another10 of 33

Read it plainly: the readers agree on both axes for roughly a fifth to a third of the rules. The first read could not move the empty cells at all, which is a weak result and not a strong one, because none of its rules sat on them. The second read could, and its placement would widen the gap by about five judgments about named rules. The honest gap is 35 on this placement and a little wider on that reader's, so a reader should treat it as a measurement of the placement and not of the law. Where the two disagree, the file keeps its first placement and says who is right in no case; each disagreement is a finding for the author.

What follows: a practice, with a place to file a defect

A map is only worth drawing if work lands on it. The practice the grid supports has six steps, each of which this site can show you:

  1. Declare the surface in a .zui file: what the person is doing, and what stays always reachable. The Language page.
  2. Place it on the grid: which activities it serves, in which channels.
  3. Let the law refuse what it refuses, by name, before anything is drawn. What it refuses.
  4. Project it to each channel; a verb a channel cannot carry is refused too. Channels.
  5. Audit what was painted. The UI audit.
  6. File every defect found against a cell, so that it confirms the grid, extends it, or falls outside it, and never becomes a new rule on its own.

The record a discovered issue is filed as, from doc/design/COVERAGE.md, so that it lands on a cell:

id:        COV-<year>-<number>        # stable, never reused
cell:      <activity> x <medium>      # one of the 66 cells, or
status:    reported | placed | confirmed | gap | extends | outside | closed
rules:     [<rule numbers on that cell the issue touches>]    # empty when the cell has none

The three landings are the point. An issue confirms a cell (a rule already forbids it, or the cell has none and stays in the gap, with a rule held for the author); it extends the grid (no row or column fits, which is proposed to the author); or it falls outside (nobody is using an interface: a maker-side concern). Evidence follows the perception gate: a defect a person would perceive stays unverified until a person has perceived it.

Limit: where these records are kept, and who files them, is not decided; the grid is the only thing fixed.
