Try · for people who must judge an application they do not own

A screenshot can decide three rules, and says so for every other one.

An application you do not own has no source to read and often no page to serve. What remains is what it shows. The screenshot audit reduces a screenshot to measurements, judges them against the rules a picture can decide, and reports everything else as not audited. It has never been run on a real application.

The writer measures, the audit judges

Two pieces, kept apart on purpose. A writer reads the screenshot and writes down what it measures: each line of text, its box, the luminances behind it, how sure the text recognition was. It decides nothing. The audit reads those facts and judges them against thresholds that live in one place. A writer that also decided "this is too small" would put the law inside a tool that has never read it.

A field the writer could not measure is empty, never a guess and never zero: a zero luminance is black text, an empty one is "I do not know", and the audit treats them differently. The shape is facts.schema.json, the reasoning is AUDIT-FACTS.md.

What it decides, and at what thresholds

RuleWhat is measuredThreshold
Rules 62 and 105, contrastthe text colour against the background behind it, at the typical and at the worst endat least 4.5 to 1, at the worst end
Rule 107, sizethe height of the line box, from the scale the caller suppliesa floor of 12 points; a tall line box proves nothing about the font inside it
Every other rulenothingnot audited, never passed

Two more thresholds keep it honest: text the recognition was less than 0.5 sure of is not judged, and a background that moves more than 0.25 toward the text at its worst end makes contrast not decided, because a photograph behind text can be fine at its typical point and unreadable at its worst. That is the limit the UI audit has on a page with a photograph, designed out here.

A run, on the example file

The checker reads a facts file, proves its shape and its arithmetic, and prints the decisions. Here on the example that ships with it, made by hand:

$ node doc/design/audit/check-facts.js doc/design/audit/example.facts.json
3 lines found; text the OCR did not find is not audited

L1 "Overdue balance"
  contrast (rules 62, 105)  fail        2.85:1 is below 4.5:1
  size floor (rule 107)     fail        the line box is 9 pt, so the font is at most that: below the 12 pt (16 CSS px) floor
L2 "Account summary"
  contrast (rules 62, 105)  pass        12.63:1 is at least 4.5:1, background uniform
  size floor (rule 107)     not_decided a 18 pt line box does not show the font is large: line height can be generous around small text
L3 "Quarterly results"
  contrast (rules 62, 105)  not_decided 4.66:1 at the typical background but 2.59:1 at the worst end (background 0.1753 moved 0.18 toward the text colour): never a pass
  size floor (rule 107)     not_decided a 18 pt line box does not show the font is large: line height can be generous around small text

NOT AUDITED: every rule other than 62, 105, 107 (rule 105 only for its contrast half). A screenshot does not decide them, and "not audited" is not "passed".
PROJECTED, NOT VERIFIED BY A PERSON (CENTRAL-PERCEPTGATE-01): nobody looked at the screenshot these facts describe.

doc/design/audit/example.facts.json: valid; 3 line(s)

One line fails twice, one passes on contrast and cannot be decided on size, and one is never a pass because its background moves toward the text at the worst end. The checker prints, on every run, that nobody looked at the screenshot these facts describe.

What has never run

  • The writer, on a real application. It belongs to another desk and runs on Ring++; it has not been run on a real application's screenshot, so this page shows the example file and not a real one.
  • The capture loop that takes a screenshot of a running application is a separate thing and is not built.
  • No person has looked at any screenshot the facts describe. The audit says so on every run, because perception is the last gate and no counter replaces it.

The same audit is the second half of the mobile workflow, where a phone app has no page to measure.
