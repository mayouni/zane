Try · for people who must judge an application they do not own

The screenshot audit judges an application you do not own on the three rules that a screenshot can decide.

The screenshot audit reduces a screenshot to measurements and judges them against the rules that a picture can decide, so that a person can audit an application whose source and pages are out of reach. An application you do not own has no source to read and often no page to serve, and only what it shows remains. The audit ensures that a rule a screenshot cannot decide is reported as not audited and never as passed. The checker decides those rules from a facts file today.

How the work is split between a writer that measures and an audit that judges

The screenshot audit has two pieces, which are kept apart on purpose. A writer reads the screenshot and writes down what it measures: each line of text, its box, the luminances behind it, and how sure the text recognition was. The writer decides nothing. The audit reads those facts and judges them against thresholds that are kept in one place. A writer that also decided that a line is too small would put the law inside a tool that has never read the law.

A field that the writer could not measure is left empty, and it is never a guess and never zero. A zero luminance means black text, an empty luminance means that the value is not known, and the audit treats the two differently. The shape of the facts is in facts.schema.json, and the reasoning is in AUDIT-FACTS.md.

The rules that the screenshot audit decides, and their thresholds

RuleWhat is measuredThreshold
Rules 62 and 105, contrastthe text colour against the background behind it, at the typical and at the worst endat least 4.5 to 1, at the worst end
Rule 107, sizethe height of the line box, from the scale the caller suppliesa floor of 12 points; a tall line box proves nothing about the font inside it
Every other rulenothingnot audited, never passed

Two more thresholds keep the audit honest. Text that the recognition was less than 0.5 sure of is not judged. A background that moves more than 0.25 toward the text at its worst end makes contrast not decided, because a photograph behind text can be readable at its typical point and unreadable at its worst. The UI audit has this limit on a page with a photograph, and the screenshot audit is designed to avoid it.

A run of the checker on the example facts file

The checker reads a facts file, proves its shape and its arithmetic, and prints the decisions. The run below uses the example file that ships with the checker, which was made by hand:

$ zane audit-facts doc/design/audit/example.facts.json
3 lines found; text the OCR did not find is not audited

L1 "Overdue balance"
  contrast (rules 62, 105)  fail        2.85:1 is below 4.5:1
  size floor (rule 107)     fail        the line box is 9 pt, so the font is at most that: below the 12 pt (16 CSS px) floor
L2 "Account summary"
  contrast (rules 62, 105)  pass        12.63:1 is at least 4.5:1, background uniform
  size floor (rule 107)     not_decided a 18 pt line box does not show the font is large: line height can be generous around small text
L3 "Quarterly results"
  contrast (rules 62, 105)  not_decided 4.66:1 at the typical background but 2.59:1 at the worst end (background 0.1753 moved 0.18 toward the text colour): never a pass
  size floor (rule 107)     not_decided a 18 pt line box does not show the font is large: line height can be generous around small text

NOT AUDITED: every rule other than 62, 105, 107 (rule 105 only for its contrast half). A screenshot does not decide them, and "not audited" is not "passed".

The writer, the capture loop and the person who looks

  • The writer reduces a screenshot to facts. It belongs to another desk and runs on Haro, and this page shows its example file.
  • The capture loop, which takes a screenshot of a running application, is a separate part.
  • A person is the last gate. The audit reports on every run whether a person has looked at the screenshot that the facts describe, because no counter replaces perception.

The same audit forms the second half of the mobile workflow, where a phone app has no page to measure.
