Try · for presenting Zane to a team

Present it in eight scenes, ordered for who is in the room.

Choose a profile and the scenes follow in the order that profile needs. The arrow keys or the space bar move between scenes, n shows the presenter's notes, f fills the screen, and Escape opens the page a scene comes from. Every number in a scene is the same number as on the site.

Who is in the room?

  • UX designer 8 scenes
  • Product designer 8 scenes
  • Programmer 8 scenes
  • AI builder 8 scenes
  • Decision maker 8 scenes

Each scene is an address: tour.html#programmer/4 opens the fourth scene for a programmer, so a presenter can start anywhere and a link can be sent.

The stage

Choose a profile above to begin.

An interface is what a person is doing, rendered.

A person does not come to click a button. They come to find something, compare, act, confirm, and undo. Zane declares that, and treats every screen as a consequence of it.

Presenter's notes. Start from the person, not the screen. Ask the room what their users come to do, and write the verbs down.

A flow you cannot leave is refused, by name.

DEFINE WIZARD account_opening (
  SATISFIES [UNDERSTAND, CONFIRM, ACT],
  ALWAYS_REACHABLE [UNDO, CANCEL],
  STEPS [identity, funding, review],
  ACTOR applicant
)
$ node tools/zane/cli.js check tour_account_opening.zane
tour_account_opening.zane:1  error  TRAPPED_FLOW  account_opening
    A multi-step flow has no reachable EXIT.
    Rule 51 · The Cognitive Tunnel · Rule 53 · Interaction Lockdown · Article II · the right to leave

1 error(s), 0 warning(s) across 1 file(s) at level 2
Presenter's notes. Point at the last line of the output: the rule, the article and the right. Nothing has been drawn yet.

The law is a file, and every refusal cites it.

123 rules under 7 articles, guaranteeing 6 rights. The tools load the same file the reader reads, so they cannot drift apart.

Honestly: only 11 rules are refused by a machine today, and 80 need a person.

Presenter's notes. The honest number is the second one. Say it before someone asks.

A map of what a person does, across every channel.

Six activities against 10 channels. Of 55 cells that can hold a rule, 20 do and 35 do not. 5 channels have no rule written for them at all.

Presenter's notes. The empty cells are the finding. A practice that cannot show where it is silent cannot be checked.

A phone line cannot zoom.

One declaration, many channels. A flow that promises ZOOM over a voice line is refused, because a voice cannot carry it, and the same flow on a screen is clean. 4 of the channels cannot carry an act at all.

Presenter's notes. This is the scene for anyone shipping voice, print or an API: the refusal is of a promise, not of the channel.

Beside heuristics, standards and frameworks, not instead of them.

Heuristics and usability tests judge what only a person can judge. Accessibility standards and linters check a rendered page. Frameworks build the screen. None of those, in what we read, refuses a declaration for lacking what a person must be able to do, before a screen exists.

Presenter's notes. Be fair to the practices in the room. The claim is narrow and the page lists its sources.

On a real site: four findings, and the instrument's own limit.

The Softanza site's navigation, read only: the verifier refused the author's own first declaration, and the audit found four pages with no labelled path. It also reported twenty-two contrast findings over a photograph, which are the audit's limit, not the site's, and the page says which is which.

Presenter's notes. Real runs, with their limits stated. That is the whole style of the project.

On Zing, in forty-seven minutes: clean.

Three surfaces declared, the verifier clean, ten pages audited, one finding on a redirect page that nobody reads. An hour tells a team where it stands.

Presenter's notes. A clean result is a result. Do not apologise for it.

In CI, a refusal is an exit code.

The verifier exits 1 on a refusal, 0 when clean and 2 on a usage error. That is all a build gate needs, and it needs Node and nothing else today.

Presenter's notes. For programmers: show the exit code, then the envelope a tool can read.

A generator can be held to a grammar, and still be refused.

A grammar file fixes the shape of a declaration (the 10 kinds and their clauses), so a model cannot write one that does not parse. It cannot fix meaning: a flow with no exit is well formed and wrong, and only the court refuses it.

Presenter's notes. For AI builders: the grammar and the court are two different promises. Say which one they need.

Agreement is proved by running the fixtures.

73 fixtures with known verdicts, and 12 files pinned by hash. A verifier in any language is a peer, and the suite says which fixture disagreed.

Presenter's notes. There is no library to adopt. That surprises people; let it.

The language is also declared as data, and two courts are held to each other.

A court forged from the declaration agrees with the hand-written verifier on 55 of 73 fixtures; the other 18 are named, and a ratchet fails on any other.

Presenter's notes. For the language people in the room. Skip it for everyone else.

What it costs today.

Node and a browser, nothing to install, no library to adopt, no framework to change. A single file that needs neither is designed and not built. The work is writing declarations, by hand, for the surfaces that matter.

Presenter's notes. For decision makers: the cost is an afternoon per surface, and the honest risk is that most of the law still needs a reviewer.

What is not shown.

No study, no user, no foreign application run end to end. The claims are structural and their evidence is the repository: files a reader can open and commands a reader can run. Nobody has perceived any channel for the grid.

Presenter's notes. Always end on the limits before the next step. It is what makes the rest believable.
