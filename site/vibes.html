The exhibit · Article VI — Operator Primacy and Vibes

Zayn treats a vibe as an interaction culture, and two cultures of one screen share one truth.

An interaction culture, which the law calls a vibe, is the way an interface presents itself to a community of operators, and the law gives it architectural respect rather than treating it as a theme. Article VI ensures that a person keeps every right in both cultures, because the vibe owns only how the screen looks and never what it shows. Below, the same operator screen is rendered in both of this site's vibes at once from one shared state. An action in either pane changes the other pane, because the truth is shared and only the projection differs. The exhibit is Article VI, running.

One state object renders both panes, so the exhibit has no synchronization code that could go wrong. The exhibit shows the projection discipline at a small scale: the vibe owns how the screen looks, and it has no freedom over what the screen shows.

What a culture may change, and what it never changes

What the vibe owns

  • Palette — indigo and rose, or phosphor on black
  • Typeface — proportional prose, or the terminal's mono
  • Density, texture, ornament
  • The medium itself — a screen, a terminal, a voice

What the vibe cannot touch

  • The six operator rights — understand, reverse, know, leave, act without fear, reach a human
  • The undo window and every declared way back (Article IV)
  • Visible state — the filter chip exists in both cultures (Article III)
  • The keyboard — every act above is a real, reachable button (Rule 80)
  • The same actions, the same labels, the same consequences

Vibes are therefore a matter of law and not of skinning. A theme changes pixels, and nobody audits what it changed. A vibe is bound by Article VI, which states that vibe-invariant rights never flex, and the UI audit measures every page of this site in both cultures before it ships, because a page can be lawful in one palette and unlawful in the other.

Where the two cultures appear outside this exhibit

Every page of this site carries the culture picker in its footer, and the F2 key flips the culture from the keyboard, which serves the operators who never touch the mouse. In the language, a culture is a declaration like any other: DEFINE VIBE is one of the ten declaration types, and this lets a compiler check that no culture has quietly dropped a right.
