The exhibit · Article VI — Operator Primacy and Vibes

Two cultures, one truth

A vibe is not a theme. It is an interaction culture — and operators' cultures deserve architectural respect. Below, the same operator screen is rendered in both of this site's vibes at once, driven by one shared state: act in either pane and the other follows, because the truth is shared and only the projection differs. That is Article VI, running.

The exhibit

Clear the filter, delete an invoice, undo it — in either pane. The other pane follows instantly. Meanwhile, press F2: the page around this exhibit flips culture, but the exhibit itself refuses to — it is showing you both at once.

One state object renders both panes — there is no synchronization code to get wrong, because there is nothing to synchronize. This is the projection discipline in miniature: the vibe owns how; it has zero freedom over what.

What may flex, and what never does

The vibe owns

  • Palette — indigo and rose, or phosphor on black
  • Typeface — proportional prose, or the terminal's mono
  • Density, texture, ornament
  • The medium itself — a screen, a terminal, a voice

The vibe cannot touch

  • The six operator rights — understand, reverse, know, leave, act without fear, reach a human
  • The undo window and every declared way back (Article IV)
  • Visible state — the filter chip exists in both cultures (Article III)
  • The keyboard — every act above is a real, reachable button (Rule 80)
  • The same actions, the same labels, the same consequences

This is why vibes are law, not skinning: a theme flexes pixels, and nobody audits what it flexed. A vibe is bound by Article VI — vibe-invariant rights never flex — and the UI audit measures every page of this site in both cultures before it ships, because a page can be lawful in one palette and unlawful in the other.

Vibes in the wild

Every page of this site carries the picker in its footer, and F2 flips culture from the keyboard — sovereignty for the operators who never touch the mouse. Zing ships the same two cultures in its own livery (teal and orange, amber terminal) — two projects, two liveries, one law. And in the language, the culture is a declaration like any other: DEFINE VIBE is one of the ten declaration types, which is what lets a compiler check that no culture quietly dropped a right.
