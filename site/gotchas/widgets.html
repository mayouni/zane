Gotchas · the widgets family

A widget is an element of an interface that plays exactly one role, and its form shows that role.

A widget is an element such as a card or a button, and its form follows its one role: a card informs, a button acts, and both answer to the keyboard. The rules of widgets let a person tell what an element does by reading it, without pressing it, and let a person reach every action from the keyboard. The two violations on this page dress widgets as each other: information that looks like a button, and a "button" that the keyboard cannot reach.

Why it is law: when every element might be pressable, the operator must press the interface to learn it, and an interface learned by pressing is never read. The rule comes from the banking product in April 2026, where an autonomy ladder was drawn as five cards that looked clickable.

The Div That Pretends: a "button" that the keyboard cannot reach Rule 80 · Keyboard Sovereignty

Each pane shows an Approve payment "button". Put the mouse away, try to reach each one with Tab, then press Enter.

The offense
Approve payment

This element is a styled <div onclick>. Tab skips past it, so an operator who uses the keyboard cannot approve the payment.

Under the law

This element is a real <button>. It takes focus, shows a visible focus ring, and responds to Enter and Space.

Why it is law: the keyboard is the operator's instrument. Any critical action that requires the mouse demotes the expert and excludes some users entirely. Conforming linters flag clickable elements that only a mouse can use, outline:none, and a positive tabindex.
