Gotchas · the widgets family

Honest affordances

Form follows role, and each widget plays exactly one: a card informs, a button acts, and both answer to the keyboard. The two offenses here dress widgets in each other's clothes — information wearing a button's costume, and a "button" the keyboard cannot even reach.

1 · The Card That Lies Rule 119 · A Card Is Not a Button

Three tiles, identically dressed. One navigates, two are information. Find out which — the only way this screen allows: poke them.

Why it is law: when everything might be pressable, the operator must poke the interface to learn it — and an interface learned by poking was never read. Born in the banking product, April 2026: an autonomy ladder drawn as five clickable-looking cards.

2 · The Div That Pretends Rule 80 · Keyboard Sovereignty

Two "buttons". Put your mouse away and try to reach them with Tab, then press Enter.

The offense
Approve payment

It's a styled <div onclick>. Tab skips straight past it — a keyboard operator simply cannot approve.

Under the law

A real <button>: focusable, visible focus ring, Enter and Space just work.

Why it is law: the keyboard is the operator's instrument; any critical act that requires the mouse demotes the expert — and excludes some users entirely. Conforming linters flag mouse-only clickables, outline:none, and positive tabindex.
