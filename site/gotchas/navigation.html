Gotchas · the navigation family — born in the 2026 harvest

Navigation is the part of an interface that lets a person move through it, and its geometry shows where the person is.

Navigation is the set of menus and tabs that lets a person move through an interface, and the rules of navigation require its geometry to carry the person's orientation. The rules let the operator read where they are from the shape of the interface, so the operator does not reconstruct it in their head. This family holds one violation today, and it grows as the harvest grows: the menu that lies about its tree (Rule 116) is already measured by the UI audit and does not have its duel yet.

The Second Row: two rows of identical tabs that hide which level owns the page Rule 120 · The Second Level Turns

The violation shows two rows of tabs, one above the other, that look the same. Click some tabs, then try to say, without reading twice, which row owns the page you are on.

Under the law

Why it is law: when navigation nests, the second level changes axis: the first level is horizontal and the second level is vertical. The turn of axis is the hierarchy, and it makes the hierarchy visible without a single label. The rule comes from the studio of the banking product in April 2026, which had two stacked rows of identical tabs that a person could not tell apart at a glance.
