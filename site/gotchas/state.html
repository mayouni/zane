Gotchas · the state family

The state of an interface is what the system knows at a given moment, and a person must be able to see it and undo changes to it.

State is what the system knows at a given moment, such as which filter is active, and the rules of state make every important part of it visible to the person. The rules ensure that a person never draws a conclusion from a state the person cannot see, and that a person can undo what the person did. The two violations on this page break each promise: a filter that silently changes what the table shows, and a deletion that questions the person twice instead of trusting the person once.

The Invisible Filter: a filter that hides rows without showing that it is active Rule 82 · Visible State

In this demo a manager asks why revenue is down. Revenue is not down: a filter that someone left on hides half of the rows.

The offense

Under the law
Sales — August filter: cash only ✕
Total

Click the chip to clear the filter. The state is always visible, and the person can always dismiss it.

Why it is law: a person who draws conclusions from a state the person cannot see has been harmed by the interface. The checker names this violation INVISIBLE_STATE, and the same article requires every norm to carry a message that a person can read.

"Are you sure?": a deletion that asks for confirmation twice instead of offering undo Rule 79 · The Undo Covenant

Press Delete in both panes. The violation questions the person before it trusts the person. The lawful twin trusts the person first and keeps the deletion reversible for ten seconds.

The offense
Invoice #204 — 45,000 XOF

A real browser dialog interrupts the person twice.

Under the law
Invoice #204 — 45,000 XOF

Why it is law: a confirmation dialog always interrupts the person and never trusts the person, while undo trusts the person first and never interrupts. Under the law, a deletion happens at once, visibly and reversibly, and the person keeps the dignity of a ten-second window to undo it. A related diagnostic of the checker, MISSING_UNDO, refuses flows that cannot state the way back.
