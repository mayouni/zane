Gotchas · the reading family — both born in the 2026 harvest

Reading is the act of scanning a table or a page, and it runs on a budget of attention that the interface must respect.

Reading covers scanning a table and reading a page, which are the same act at two speeds, and the rules of reading keep both within the reader's budget of attention. The rules give the eye one place to look, and they require a page to earn each of its sections. The two violations on this page overspend the budget: a table in which one colour means two things, and a page that spends the reader's memory faster than it earns the reader's attention. An AI committed both violations on a real application before they became law.

Where to go deeper

Each section above links out. Depth is below the fold, not in your way.

Why it is law: a reading surface has a budget of roughly ten to twelve sections, ordered from general to specific, with value a person can skim at the top, depth below, and links out for the rest. A paragraph of more than eight hundred characters without a break is a wall, and it must become a lead plus a link. A reader who has met twenty sections remembers none of them. The rule comes from the Refine rebuild in June 2026.
