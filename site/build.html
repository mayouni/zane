Declare before you draw

Before wireframes harden, write the surface's .zui declaration: which intents it satisfies, what stays always reachable. A few minutes of declaration replaces a meeting of taste: the design review reads SATISFIES [SELECT, CONFIRM, ACT] and asks one question, is that what the operator is actually doing?, instead of debating paddings.

// specs/checkout.zui — written at the whiteboard, checked at the desk
DEFINE WIZARD checkout (
  SATISFIES [SELECT, CONFIRM, ACT],
  ALWAYS_REACHABLE [CANCEL, EXIT]
)
$ node tools/zui/cli.js check checkout.zui
checkout.zui:2  error  MISSING_UNDO  checkout
    An act declares no way back: SATISFIES holds ACT, ALWAYS_REACHABLE lacks UNDO.
    Rule 79 · The Undo Covenant · Article IV · the right to reverse

1 error(s), 0 warning(s) across 1 file(s) at level 2

That is the output of a real run, and this site's own check re-runs it. The wizard has an exit and a way to cancel, and the law still refuses it, because it acts and promises no undo. The verifier ships in this repository and runs on Node alone. It reads its contract and its citations from the law rather than hardcoding them.

What a declaration alone can decide is level two, the default. A check that needs a fact a declaration does not hold (the entities, the actors, the state, the elements of a surface) is skipped unless you hand it a symbol table with --symbols: undecidable is silence, never a pass. Computing that table from a host project is not built (see the last section).

Pin the law as a hash, not only a version

A version says which edition you meant. A hash says the bytes you hold are that edition. Vendor the files the protocol manifest names (the law, the language documents, the refusal contract, the two axes and the proof), record protocol/MANIFEST.json beside them, and make one command a step of your build:

$ node tools/zui/pack.js --check

Then conforms to Zui v3.13 is a claim your CI can refute. Numbers are stable forever: a retired number is never reused, so a finding that cites Rule 79 means the same thing in ten years. The protocol pack says what to vendor and what you may and may not claim.

Fail the build on a refusal

The verifier exits non-zero when any declaration is refused, and zero when there are only warnings. Run it over your specifications in CI:

$ node tools/zui/cli.js check specs/*.zui
$ node tools/zui/cli.js check specs/*.zui --json   # for a machine to read

The contract names 37 diagnostics at 4 conformance levels, and every finding carries the rule, article or right it rests on. The suite that proves any implementation agrees, 73 fixtures with known verdicts, is in conformance/; Zing is the first conforming consumer.

Measure the paint

Declaring is not enough: a page can be lawful on paper and unlawful in the browser. Serve your application and point the UI audit at it, one path per line, any same-origin pages; nothing leaves your browser. It measures what static analysis cannot see: computed text sizes, true contrast against the composited background, the trail, the room before scrollbars, the navigation bars against each other and the menu against the tree. A colour it cannot read is reported as not decided, naming the colour, and never skipped. It exports a Markdown report, each finding citing its rule.

Run it in every culture you ship: a page can be lawful in one palette and unlawful in another. This site audits itself that way before it ships.

Amend with evidence

The law grows one way only (Article VII): real usage, operator feedback, documented failure. If your project hits a recurring defect the law does not name, record it as the law records its own: what happened, where, how it was corrected, why it generalizes, and whether a reviewer could decide it yes or no. Every rule carries an evidence field saying where it came from, and fashion, trends and developer convenience are not admissible. The amendment docket shows how a pass is ruled.

What is not here yet

Stated as held or designed, not as promised.

  • Cross-pillar symbols are not computed. The verifier accepts a symbol table you supply; building one from a host project's other pillars is the open half of level three, unchanged.
  • One violation belongs to the audit, not the verifier. LAYOUT_DISPLACEMENT is a fact about a painted page, so it has no fixture by design.
  • The AI-interaction jurisdiction is not written: no rules for the chat stream, and no endpoint that serves the law to an agent while it works. The roadmap keeps the account.
  • The Second Founding is proposed, not ratified. It argues for a wider design and its own rulings; the law you pin is the one above, not that document.
