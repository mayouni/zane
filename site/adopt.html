The contract · consume the law, today

Put your UI under law

The law is one JSON file with a stable schema. You do not need a framework, a plugin, or permission — you need to pin a version, check against it, and cite rule numbers. Here is the whole contract, and what you can do with it this afternoon.

1 · Pin a version

Vendor rules.json into your repository and record the version you pinned — exactly as a runtime pins a conformance-fixtures file. Your build now has a fact, not an opinion: conforms to Zui v3.7. StzWeb, the first conforming consumer, pins v3.6 until it re-pins on its own verification.

$ curl -O https://…/constitution/rules.json
$ git add vendor/zui/rules.json
$ git commit -m "pin Zui v3.7"

Numbers are stable forever — retired numbers are never reused, burned numbers stay burned. A finding that cites Rule 79 will mean the same thing in ten years.

2 · Audit what you render

Serve your application and point the UI audit at it — one path per line, any same-origin pages. It measures the painted page: computed text sizes, true composited contrast, the trail, the room before scrollbars, the menu against the tree. It exports the findings as a Markdown report, each violation citing its rule number. Nothing leaves your browser.

Run it in both of your themes if you have them — a page can be lawful in one palette and unlawful in the other. This site audits itself, in both vibes, before every page ships; the audit once caught its own defect doing so.

3 · Feed it to your generator

This works today, with any capable AI assistant: put rules.json in its context and instruct it to obey the law and cite rule numbers in its output. Then close the loop: run the UI audit on what it built, and paste the Markdown report back. The generator now has what it never had before — a written law it can be held to, and a measurement it cannot argue with.

You are building under the Zui Constitution (rules.json attached).
Every screen you produce must conform. Cite the rule numbers you
obeyed. Here is the audit report of your last attempt — fix every
finding, by number: 2 violations [Rule 105, Rule 114]

The designed endgame (M5) is an MCP surface serving the law to AI tools natively, so generators consult the constitution while generating and declare what they obeyed. Until it ships, the context-window contract above is the honest workaround — and it already changes what generators produce.

4 · Amend with evidence

The law grows one way only (Article VII): real usage, operator feedback, documented failure. If your project hits a recurring defect the law does not name, record it the way the harvest records were kept — what happened, where, how it was corrected, why it generalizes, and whether a reviewer could decide it yes-or-no. That record is an amendment candidate. Fashion, trends, and developer convenience are not admissible; every rule above 104 entered exactly this way.

The road

What adoption will look like as the instruments land — stated as design, not as promise: M3, a standalone zui verifier CLI lifted from Zin's reference implementation, with conformance fixtures so any implementation in any language can prove agreement. M4, the intent→widget projection tables as data, checkable like the rules themselves. M5, the AI-interaction jurisdiction: laws for the chat stream, consent surfaces, and the MCP endpoint that serves all of it to generators. The details live in the roadmap.
