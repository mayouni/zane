The decay you already know

You have used this software. Small faint text you lean into. Buttons labeled "OK" that do not say what they will do. A chat launcher floating over the paragraph you were reading. A breadcrumb that shows where you came from but not where you could go. A table that scrolls sideways next to twenty centimetres of empty page. A menu that lists a page as top-level while the page's own path says it lives two levels deep.

None of this is new. These are inherited conventions: patterns copied because they are everywhere, and kept because nothing in the process is required to refuse them. Each one taxes the person using the screen, a little attention here, a little confidence there, for hours every day.

What the record shows

The law in this project was not written in a seminar room. Its newest rules were extracted, defect by defect, from real applications built with AI assistance in 2026, each correction recorded when it was made and kept only if it generalised and a reviewer could decide it yes or no. Rules 105 to 124 each come from such a defect, and the table further down names the newest eight.

The working thesis is a narrow one: in those builds, a generator reproduced the inherited conventions quickly and confidently, under an instruction to be careful, because nothing it was given had the standing to refuse them. The only correction that keeps pace with a generator is a rule the generator can be made to check.

Limit: this is the record of one author's builds. It shows that these defects occurred and were corrected; it does not show how common they are in other teams' work.

What changes when the law is data

Guidelines in prose are good at what they do, and the Positioning page says where each stops. What a guideline cannot do alone is say no to a build. Storing the law as one versioned file changes that in five ways, and each one can be checked:

The claimWhere to check it
The Reader that shows the law and the verifier that refuses for it load the same file, so they cannot drift apart.The Reader, and one rule as the tools load it.
A finding cites a rule, an article and a right, so a review is an audit and not an argument.What it refuses.
A consumer pins a version and a hash, so conformance is a fact about a build.Conformance.
An amendment needs documented evidence, never fashion (Article VII).How it changes.
Retired numbers are never reused, so a citation means the same for ever.How it changes, and the lineage line in rules.json.

The law also has a home that prose lacks: a language. Interfaces are declared in StzZui, what the person is doing and what stays reachable, and the verifier refuses unlawful structure before anything is drawn, while the UI audit measures what was painted. The Language page walks the four steps.

What a law cannot do

  • Most of it waits on a person. Only 11 of the 123 rules are refused by a conforming compiler today; 80 need a reviewer's judgment.
  • It does not tell you the page is pleasant. The verifier checks a declaration and the audit checks what was painted. Whether a person finds it usable is for usability testing, which nothing here replaces.
  • It is not shown to be right for other teams. No study, no user and no foreign application run end to end stand behind it, only this repository.
  • It has a stance. Business applications first, below.

The story: a year of building with AI

Every correction below was recorded at the moment it was made, then canonized only if it generalised and could be decided yes or no by a reviewer.

the banking product (April 2026) — a full agentic banking platform, built and corrected screen by screen. Supervision tables where the same green meant two different things two columns apart. Card-styled tiles you had to poke to learn. Two stacked rows of identical tabs. A floating assistant masking the very work it offered to help with.

A sibling project's i18n work (May 2026) — languages added by forking templates, leaving French screens full of untranslated jargon, until language became data. The Refine rebuild (June 2026) — a 22-section homepage the author judged unreadable, rebuilt under a reading budget.

The StzWeb site (August 2026) — twelve rules, from Rule 105 through 116, each canonized live from a defect the generator produced while building the site that documents the law. The audit tool then caught its own depth-calculation defect on its first run in this repository. Nothing here is exempt, including the tooling.

The stance

Opinionated, and openly so: business applications first. Operators, not explorers — people who use the same screens eight hours a day under pressure, responsibility, and fatigue. Consumer-site delight is out of jurisdiction; operator dignity is the whole of it. The law's own preamble says it best:

Calm is productive. Stability is humane. Clarity is respectful. Undo is dignity.
— The Zui Constitution, preamble
