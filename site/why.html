Why · the problem Zane answers

The problem Zane answers is that interfaces built with AI assistants repeat the same defects, even after explicit instruction.

The problem is measured on the author's own work: 455 corrections of interfaces in 26 projects, 164 of them a defect that had been corrected before. This page states the problem, its cost, the decision taken, the state of interfaces built with AI, and what Zane does about it.

The same interface defects return after every instruction

An AI model builds an interface from the interfaces it learned from. It reproduces their conventions: small grey text for what matters less, boxes and rules to make sections, a width stretched to fill the screen, a word left alone on the last line of a title.

It does so with no intention. The corpus is what the model knows, so an instruction corrects one page and the convention returns on the next. The author named the cost in these words:

"what makes my fatigue in all my projects is never the more complex technical challenges: it's always UI!"
— the author of Zane, 2026-11-09

What the defects cost, counted in the corpus of corrections

The corpus gathers every UI correction the author made, from the memory of each project and from the repositories themselves: 455 corrections of something a person sees, touches or hears, in 26 projects, from February to November 2026.

  • Repeated. 164 corrections fix a defect that had been corrected before, in the same project or in another.
  • Concentrated. On 9 November 2026 alone the author made 42 corrections on two sites.
  • Classified. Every correction belongs to one of 13 root causes: the default the generator follows when nothing stops it.

The corpus also keeps the author's words of repetition: "I told you a thousand times", "as usual: text is very small and unreadable!!!", "how many times I told you to avoid this ugly word taking its own line!!!". Time, effort and patience went to corrections that a rule could have made once.

Every record, with its source, is in the corpus.

The decision to resolve the problem from first principles

The author decided to resolve the problem as everything in Softanza is resolved: from first principles. A correction stored as a symptom moves the defect to the next page; a rule stated as its cause, bounded on both sides and checked on the painted page, ends it.

The design system rests on six laws of perception, attention, action and language, each measured long before these sites existed:

  • Grouping comes before reading. The eye groups what is near, alike or enclosed before it reads a word.
  • Every visible difference is read as a meaning. A difference that means nothing is noise.
  • Attention and memory are scarce. Emphasis is a budget, and a choice costs time.
  • Acting is a loop that closes through what is perceived. A shape tells what can be done, and the page shows what happened.
  • Language is cooperative. A reader expects as much as is needed, plainly said.
  • The senses have floors. Below a size, a contrast or a target, reading and touching fail.

The design system derives its principles from these laws, and the samples page shows each one.

Interfaces built with AI carry the same defects into everyday software

The author observes the same defects beyond these projects. AI tools, Claude Desktop among them, the tool the author uses, and the tools that share their interface style are themselves built with AI, and they carry the conventions of the corpus into the software people use every day.

UI and UX specialists have stayed silent about it. Graphical interaction is entering a crisis, and the corpus above is the author's evidence for it.

What Zane does about the problem

Zane turns each correction into something a machine can refuse. An interface is declared as what the user is doing; the verifier refuses an unlawful declaration before anything is drawn and cites the rule; the UI audit measures the painted page against the design system's checks.

The Language section shows the four steps, the Law section holds the rules, and the Demo section runs them.

The stance of the law, which puts business applications first

The law is openly opinionated and puts business applications first. It serves operators: people who use the same screens eight hours a day under pressure, responsibility and fatigue. The dignity of the operator is its whole concern, and its preamble states the stance in four sentences:

Calm is productive. Stability is humane. Clarity is respectful. Undo is dignity.
— The Zane Constitution, preamble
