The case · why a law, not another guideline

Interfaces are decaying. Guidelines didn't stop it. A law can.

This page makes the argument in full: what is decaying, why the AI era turned a chronic problem into an acute one, why forty years of guidelines failed to hold the line — and what is structurally different about Zui's answer: a compilable language for intent, backed by a constitution written as data.

The decay you already know

You have used this software. Small faint text you lean into. Buttons labeled "OK" that do not say what they will do. A chat launcher floating over the paragraph you were reading. A breadcrumb that shows where you came from but not where you could go. A table that scrolls sideways next to twenty centimetres of empty page. A menu that lists a page as top-level while the page's own path says it lives two levels deep.

None of this is an accident of taste, and none of it is new. These are inherited conventions — patterns copied for decades because they are everywhere, kept because nothing is required to refuse them. Each one taxes the person using the screen: a little attention here, a little confidence there, eight hours a day.

The amplifier

Then generators arrived, trained on that corpus. An AI asked to build a business interface reproduces the corpus's every inherited assumption — politely, confidently, at machine speed. It does not do this because it is careless. It does it because nothing it was given has the standing to refuse.

The claim is recorded, not rhetorical: every rule numbered above 104 in this constitution was canonized from a defect an AI produced while building a real application — under explicit instruction to be careful. The evidence table below names each defect and the project it was committed in.

This is the thesis of StzZui: in the AI era, UX is the first casualty — and the only correction that scales at the generator's speed is a law the generator can be made to check.

The abdication

The discipline that once policed these things has largely gone quiet. Much of the HCI and UI/UX community is riding the "AI will replace us" wave — producing commentary about tools rather than restating first principles. The principles did not stop being true. They stopped being written down in a form anything could enforce.

Someone has to write them down in a form a machine can refuse to violate. That is the whole project.

Why guidelines fail, and law doesn't

Guidelines are prose. Prose advises; a reviewer negotiates; a deadline wins. A guideline drifts silently from what anyone actually checks, and no two teams inherit the same reading of it.

The Zui Constitution is data — one versioned JSON file. What follows structurally is what prose can never guarantee: linters load the same file the reader renders, so documentation and enforcement cannot drift apart. Findings cite rule numbers, so a review is an audit, not an argument. Consumers pin a version, so conformance is a fact about a build. Amendment requires documented evidence — real usage, operator feedback, recorded failure — never fashion (Article VII). And retired numbers are never reused, so a citation means the same thing forever.

And the law has a native habitat prose never had: a language. Interfaces are declared in Zui — what the human is doing, what stays reachable — and the compiler refuses unlawful structure before a pixel exists. A law that cannot be measured will be negotiated in the hallway; this one is compiled at design time and measured at runtime. How it works covers the whole pipeline.

The story: a year of building with AI

This law was not written in a seminar room. It was extracted, defect by defect, from real applications built with AI assistance through 2026 — every correction recorded at the moment it was made, then canonized only if it generalized and could be decided yes-or-no by a reviewer.

the banking product (April 2026) — a full agentic banking platform, built and corrected screen by screen. Supervision tables where the same green meant two different things two columns apart. Card-styled tiles you had to poke to learn. Two stacked rows of identical tabs. A floating assistant masking the very work it offered to help with.

A sibling project's i18n work (May 2026) — languages added by forking templates, leaving French screens full of untranslated jargon, until language became data. The Refine rebuild (June 2026) — a 22-section homepage the author judged unreadable, rebuilt under a reading budget.

The StzWeb site (August 2026) — twelve rules, 105 through 116, each canonized live from a defect the generator produced while building the site that documents the law. The audit tool then caught its own depth-calculation defect on its first run in this repository. Nothing here is exempt, including the tooling.

The stance

Opinionated, and openly so: business applications first. Operators, not explorers — people who use the same screens eight hours a day under pressure, responsibility, and fatigue. Consumer-site delight is out of jurisdiction; operator dignity is the whole of it. The law's own preamble says it best:

Calm is productive. Stability is humane. Clarity is respectful. Undo is dignity.
— The Zui Constitution, preamble
