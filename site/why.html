Why · the problem behind Zayn, and Softanza's answer

Zayn exists for one problem: the interface was always the hardest part of building software with AI, and Softanza answers it from first principles.

The author of Zayn met the problem over years of projects built with AI assistants, and counted it: 480 corrections of interfaces in 26 projects, 170 of them a defect corrected before. Softanza answers it with a system-design approach to intent-driven UI and a compiled language that governs the interface, for people and for agents.

The interface was the hard part of every project built with AI

The author of Zayn has built software with AI assistants for years, across many projects: business applications, a programming environment, public sites and products made for clients. In every one of them, the interface was the hardest part to get right.

  • The most nerve-wracking part. A defect in the interface is seen at once, by every user, and the same defect came back after it was corrected.
  • The most costly part. The time went to correcting what a person sees, touches or hears, far more than to the logic behind it.
  • The part where AI is least capable. An assistant that solves a hard technical problem still sets a reading text too small, stretches a box across the screen, or cuts a title across its sentences.

The author named it in these words:

"what makes my fatigue in all my projects is never the more complex technical challenges: it's always UI!"
— the author of Zayn, 2026-11-09

What the interface cost, counted in the author's own corrections

The corpus gathers every correction of an interface the author made, from the memory of each project and from the repositories themselves: 480 corrections in 26 projects, from February to November 2026.

  • Repeated. 170 corrections fix a defect that had been corrected before, in the same project or in another.
  • Concentrated. On 9 November 2026 alone the author made 42 corrections on two sites.
  • Classified. Every correction belongs to one of 13 root causes: the default an assistant follows when nothing stops it.

The corpus also keeps the author's words of repetition: "I told you a thousand times", "as usual: text is very small and unreadable!!!", "how many times I told you to avoid this ugly word taking its own line!!!". Every record, with its source, is in the corpus.

Why the interface is where an AI assistant is least capable

An assistant builds an interface from the interfaces it learned from, and it reproduces their conventions with no intention: small grey text for what matters less, boxes and rules to make sections, a width stretched to fill the screen. An instruction corrects one page, and the convention returns on the next, because the corpus is what the assistant knows.

An assistant also checks what it can read, the structure of the page, and a defect of the interface lives in what is painted: its size, its place, its line, its balance. A page can pass every test and still be wrong to the eye.

The same defects reach everyday software: AI tools, Claude Desktop among them, are built with AI and carry the conventions of the corpus into the interfaces people use every day. UI and UX specialists have stayed silent about it, and graphical interaction is entering a crisis.

Softanza's mission: technology rethought from first principles

Softanza, the family of projects Zayn belongs to, rethinks technology from first principles: it starts from what is true of the problem, not from the conventions that grew around it. Zayn applies this to the interface. A correction stored as a symptom moves the defect to the next page; a rule stated as its cause, bounded on both sides and checked on the painted page, ends it.

The design system rests on six laws of perception, attention, action and language, each measured long before these sites existed:

  • Grouping comes before reading. The eye groups what is near, alike or enclosed before it reads a word.
  • Every visible difference is read as a meaning. A difference that means nothing is noise.
  • Attention and memory are scarce. Emphasis is a budget, and a choice costs time.
  • Acting is a loop that closes through what is perceived. A shape tells what can be done, and the page shows what happened.
  • Language is cooperative. A reader expects as much as is needed, plainly said.
  • The senses have floors. Below a size, a contrast or a target, reading and touching fail.

The design system derives its principles from these laws, and the samples page shows each one.

A system-design approach to intent-driven UI

Zayn treats the interface as a system to design, not a screen to draw. A designer declares what the user is doing: discovering, comparing, acting, undoing. The widgets follow from that intent, and they are never where the design starts.

  • One declaration, many channels. The same declaration serves a screen, a voice line or an agent, and each channel carries the verbs it can.
  • One vocabulary, closed. The design system gives the generator a fixed set of sizes, spaces, shapes and components, so an interface is composed from parts that are already lawful.
  • One law, at every moment. The declaration is checked at design time, linted in development and measured at runtime.

A compiled language that masters the interface and governs it, for people and for agents

Zayn is a language that compiles. Its law is one versioned file of 123 rules, and the verifier refuses an unlawful declaration before anything is drawn, citing the rule it breaks. The UI audit then measures the painted page against the design system's checks.

The user of an interface is a person or an agent. The same structure a person sees is readable by an agent: landmarks, one title, headings in order, labelled fields. Zayn governs both, so an interface that serves people also serves the agents that act for them.
