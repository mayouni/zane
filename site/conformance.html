Language · for people who build a tool that reads Zane

Conformance is the proof that a tool gives the reference verifier's verdict on every fixture, and any CI can refute a false claim to it.

A tool conforms to Zane when it gives the reference verifier's verdict on every fixture of the suite, and conformance is the test that lets the maker of a tool prove that agreement. Conformance ensures that a claim to read Zane can be refuted by running two commands in any CI. The suite holds 77 fixtures, judged against a pack of 12 files pinned by hash. There is no library to adopt, because agreement is proved by running the fixtures. This page shows the two commands, what they cannot say, and the status of every part of the system.

The two commands that check the pack and run the conformance suite

The first command checks whether the bytes a person holds are the intended pack. A version number states which edition was intended, and the hash proves that the bytes are that edition. The second command runs every fixture through the verifier and reports which fixtures, if any, disagree.

$ zane pack --check

The Zane protocol pack
  law          v3.16 — 123 rules, 17 forbidden patterns
  language     v1.6 — 40 diagnostics
  axes         10 media, 5 semantic values
  proof        77 fixtures + the cross-medium spec
  pinned       12 files, sha256 over LF-normalised content
  README       every count on the page matches the manifest

  verified — every pinned file matches the manifest
$ zane conformance

Zane conformance — level 4
  spec         doc/language/ v1.6
  contract     violations.json v1.10 (constitution v3.16)
  envelope     diagnostic-contract v1.2.1 — 59 diagnostics and 77 reports, all conform
  media        10 media — verifier agrees with media.json
  catalogue    40 diagnostics — contract agrees with VIOLATIONS.md
  fixtures     77 total
  pass 77 · fail 0 · skipped 0 (above level 4)

  conforms at level 4 — every fixture in the suite.

This site's own check re-runs both outputs above, so the page cannot drift from the tools.

What "level 4" means here. The suite holds 12 fixtures at level one, 60 at level two, 5 at level three and 0 at level four. A claim at level four means that every fixture the suite holds passes. By design, the suite holds no fixture above level three, because the audit measures a painted layout and a declaration does not. A reader should therefore read a level-four claim as a level-three claim.

What a passing conformance run does not prove

  • A pass proves agreement, not rightness. Two tools that refuse the same wrong thing pass together. The suite fixes what the law refuses, and whether the law is right for a person is a question of perception, which a person decides.
  • A pass covers only what the fixtures cover. 3 diagnostics have no fixture on purpose, and the manifest gives the reason for each: one is about a painted layout, and two are about recovering from a malformed file, which the language does not define. A tool can pass the suite and differ on these diagnostics.
  • A twin proves that a tool does not over-refuse. The suite holds 23 lawful twins, which are files that differ from a refused file by one change. A tool that refuses everything fails them.
  • A pass does not make a tool a port. A port also states its level, pins the manifest, emits the refusal envelope, and lists where it differs from the reference in the way that the forged court does.

The status of each part of the system, and where each part stops

Each row names a part, gives one word for its status, and says where the part stops. Exists means that the part is built and run in this repository, or named as run elsewhere. Designed means that the part is written down as a design. Held means that the part is deliberately not advanced and awaits a named reason.

The checks this site runs on itself, and the consumers of the law

This site aims to obey the law it publishes. Before each piece of the site shipped, its author ran the UI audit in both cultures and recorded the result with the commit, but no gate enforces this audit yet.

The F2 key, or the Theme select at the top of any page, switches the culture, and the rights of the person stay the same in both cultures (Article VI). What a violation feels like lets a person commit each family of offence inside a safe fence, beside its lawful twin, and the two-cultures exhibit renders one shared truth in both cultures at once.

The consumers of the law are named here with their figures. The reference implementation is tools/zane, and this site's own check (tools/site-facts.js) re-runs it on 18 declarations shown on these pages and fails if the page and the verifier ever disagree.

What this site stands on, and the stance of the law

  • The coverage grid is designed and measured: every count on it is computed from the law and the suite.
  • The site rests on the ratified law. The wider design, the Second Founding, is a proposal beside it.
  • Rules by channel: the rules for haptic, luminous, signage, spatial and terminal channels are written when a product that ships in one of them is named.

The stance of the law is openly opinionated. It puts business applications first and serves operators rather than explorers. It prefers calm to power, intent to mechanics, and the visible to the hidden, and it treats undo as a matter of dignity.
