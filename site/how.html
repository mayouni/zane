How the language works

Most UI frameworks begin with a physical assumption — screens, widgets, layouts — and try to infer what the user intends. Zui inverts the order: you declare human intent, and rendering is a consequence. Once intent is source code, interface correctness is compilable: violations are refused before the interface exists, cited while it is built, and measured after it is painted. One declaration, one law, every phase of the project.

The pipeline

1 · Declare

In design, before a pixel: .zui files state what the human is doing on each surface — flows, forms, screens, worlds. The declaration is the design document, and it compiles.

2 · Check

The compiler refuses unlawful structure by name — ten named violations — and checks the declaration against the constitution. Findings cite rule numbers, not opinions.

3 · Project

A projection renders the checked declaration to its medium — business web, CLI, voice, game world, agent surface. It owns how; it has zero freedom over what.

4 · Measure

At runtime, the UI audit measures the painted page — computed sizes, true contrast, the trail, the room, the menu against the tree — what static analysis cannot see.

The language

Ten declaration types cover the interface's whole life: INTENT_FLOW · FORM · WIZARD · SCREEN · COMPONENT · NAV_GRAPH · WORLD_VIEW · INTERACTION · FEEDBACK · VIBE. This is a real declaration, compiling today in the reference implementation:

// Restolean — Human Interface Layer (Zui)
DEFINE INTENT_FLOW order_flow_intent (
  SATISFIES [ACT, CONFIRM, SELECT],
  ALWAYS_REACHABLE [UNDO, CANCEL]
)

DEFINE FORM new_order_form (
  INTENT_FLOW order_flow_intent
)

DEFINE WORLD_VIEW kitchen_dashboard (
  PERSPECTIVE top_down,
  VISIBLE_ENTITIES [order, dish, ingredient],
  SATISFIES [NAVIGATE]
)

Declarations quantify over a closed grammar of 24 verbs — a new medium adds renderings, never verbs. Every UI element must trace to at least one intent; an orphan element is a compile error.

Orienting

DISCOVER · UNDERSTAND · LOCATE

Discovery must be satisfiable at every entry point.

Attention

FOCUS · FILTER · COMPARE

An active filter is visible state; focus and discovery cannot both be at maximum.

Information

READ · SCAN · SCROLL · ZOOM

A read surface that silently acts is an intent violation.

Selection

SELECT · HIGHLIGHT · PREVIEW · MARK

Selection expresses interest, never commitment.

Action

ACT · CONFIRM · CANCEL · UNDO

Every act has a reachable undo, or declares why it cannot.

Continuity

PAUSE · RESUME · RETRY · EXIT

Pause, resume, and exit are always reachable; a trapped flow is unconstitutional.

Compilation: refusal by name

Two intent collapses are structural errors, not style choices: SELECT → ACT without CONFIRM (selection that executes), and READ → ACT in any form (a reading surface that mutates). They belong to a contract of ten named violations that any conforming implementation must detect:

$ zui check src/model/restaurant.zui
INTENT_ORPHAN        an element traces to no intent
SELECT_ACT_COLLAPSE  selection executes without CONFIRM
READ_ACT_COLLAPSE    a reading surface mutates
MISSING_UNDO         an act declares no way back
MISSING_CANCEL       a flow cannot be abandoned
TRAPPED_FLOW         no reachable EXIT
INVISIBLE_STATE      the system knows; the operator can't see
HIDDEN_AUTHORITY     a consequential act names no actor
LAYOUT_DISPLACEMENT  arriving content shoves the reading
DECORATIVE_ANIMATION motion that reports nothing

Status, plainly: the grammar above parses today in the reference implementation (parser.zig, inside the Zin platform — a consumer of this language), whose semantic.zig enforces four Zui diagnostics: an INTENT_FLOW without SATISFIES, an invalid PERSPECTIVE, an invalid TONE, and an unresolved INTENT_FLOWS reference. The ten named violations are the specified contract, not yet implemented — they are M3's work, now open with a decision document. Six of the ten are decidable from a single declaration (SATISFIES and ALWAYS_REACHABLE are written in the file); four need a symbol table from other pillars, which is the crux that decision weighs.

The semantic layer: a constitution as data

What makes the language refuse is not taste — it is one JSON file: v3.7, 117 rules in 31 sets under 7 articles. Every rule carries the same anatomy: what to avoid, the lawful solution, the why, the article it derives from, its enforcement tier, and the real-world evidence that earned it a number. Here is Rule 117, exactly as the tooling loads it:

{
  "number": "117",
  "title": "One Column Carries the Colour",
  "avoid": ["Several coloured columns competing in one table row",
            "The same green meaning two different things two columns apart", …],
  "solution": "Colour is a scarce signalling resource, and a table spends
     it in exactly one place: the actionable-status column…",
  "why": "A signal that appears everywhere is a signal that means nothing.
     The operator scanning two hundred rows at hour seven deserves one
     place to look.",
  "derived_from": "calm",
  "enforcement": "judgment",
  "evidence": "ZinBank supervision tables (April 2026): …"
}

The articles are the axioms — Calm, Intent Over Mechanics, Visible State, Reversibility, Predictable Authority, Operator Primacy & Vibes, Amendment — and the rules are their case law. Because the law is data, the reader renders it, the compiler checks it, findings cite it, and no prose can drift from what the machine enforces.

The enforcement ladder

Every rule declares who can currently catch its violation, and rules climb as instruments are invented — Rule 107 began as judgment and is now machine-checked; 117 and 118 are next (colour-column counting is measurable). The census, computed from the file:

The rights and the forbidden

Across the rules, the law guarantees six operator rights — to understand, to reverse, to know, to leave, to act without fear, and to a human — each traceable to the specific rules that enforce it. And it names fifteen forbidden patterns outright: modal storms, invisible active filters, hidden destructive actions, selection that silently executes, confirm-shaming, pre-checked consent, mouse-only critical workflows, decorative animation, layout shifts during interaction, auto-committed configuration, accusatory errors, unmarked machine-made content, instant reset after a critical action, "everything" as the default view, and the app closing by surprise. The Reader renders all of it, live from the same file.

Projections: any medium, zero discretion over rights

A projection maps a checked declaration to a concrete medium: business web, CLI/TUI, voice, game worlds — and, for the first time, the AI-agent surface itself: the chat stream, the tool-approval prompt, the generated artifact. Rule 121 (the assistant never masks the work) and Rule 124 (the generator ships a working thing) are the first laws written for that medium; a full chat-stream jurisdiction is in design.

The discipline is one sentence: a projection owns how, never what. It may choose typography, widgets, pacing, even whether the medium has a screen at all. It may not define a new verb, hide an available action, drop a declared UNDO, or strip EXIT from a flow. Two projections of one declaration are two cultures of one truth — press F2 on this site to feel it.

One law, three moments — and the status board

The same law holds at design time (the declaration is checked before a pixel exists), in development (every build is linted, every finding cites a number, generators are held to the file), and at runtime (the audit measures what was actually painted).
