The claim, and what bounds it

Practices for judging an interface after it exists are well established, and so are vocabularies for building one. In the pages listed here, read on 4 November 2026, we found no account of what an interface must let a person do, across a set of channels, that a machine can refuse a declaration for lacking before the interface exists. StzZui offers one: six things a person does, across 10 channels, with the law's 123 rules placed on the cells and the empty cells drawn empty.

That is a statement about the sources below, not about the field. Other work may exist that we did not find. It is also a structural claim, and its evidence is this repository: there is no study, no user and no foreign application run end to end. A reader who knows of a practice that already does this is asked to say so.

Eight practices, from their own pages

Practice, and its pageWhat it decides, and whenWhat StzZui takesWhat StzZui adds
Ten usability heuristics
Nielsen Norman Group		 Presented as ten broad rules of thumb for interaction design, first developed for heuristic evaluation, where reviewers judge an interface against them. The page names no pass or fail. The ground. Control and freedom, and error prevention, cover the same ground as the law's undo, cancel and exit rules (Rules 79, 26 and 51). Refusal. A declaration that lacks a way back is refused by name before anything is built. The heuristics still judge what only a reviewer can.
WCAG 2.2
W3C Recommendation		 Testable success criteria at three levels, for access by people with disabilities, checked by a mix of automated testing and human evaluation. The idea that a criterion is testable. The audit measures contrast on the painted page, and Rules 62 and 105 set a 4.5:1 floor; the rules do not cite WCAG, and this page does not claim they implement it. Intent and rights beyond access: a flow with no exit, an act with no undo. It is not a conformance claim to WCAG.
Design systems
Material 3, Apple HIG, Fluent 2		 Guidelines, components and tools for building an interface; Apple's guide is organised as foundations, patterns for common tasks, and components. They are applied by people while designing and building. Patterns and principles as the shape of good practice. The same concern stated as a declaration a machine reads before a widget is chosen. The pages we read describe aids such as accessibility checks inside design tools, and no checker that refuses a design for lacking an intent or a right.
Design tokens
Design Tokens Community Group		 A draft JSON format for exchanging named values such as colours and sizes between tools. It says it is not a W3C standard. The habit of making a decision data, so tools share one source. Data for the intent and the rights, not for the look: the law and the media carriage are files a tool loads.
Component libraries and Storybook
Storybook		 Building, showing, testing and documenting components and screens apart from the application. Examples a person can run beside their description, as the lawful twins do. A place to say what a person is doing with a component. The page we read does not describe one.
Accessibility linters
axe-core		 Rules run against rendered pages and HTML for WCAG 2.0 to 2.2. It states that it finds a little over half of WCAG issues automatically and reports the cases it cannot judge as incomplete, for a person to review. The honesty: a case it cannot judge reads incomplete, never passed. The screenshot audit reads not audited the same way. A law behind each finding, cited by number, and checks on the declaration before there is markup. The UI audit measures six rules, a much narrower set than axe-core's.
Usability testing
digital.gov		 Measuring how easily people reach their goals with a service, by established research methods, tracking success and satisfaction. Nothing replaces it. No instrument here perceives for a person. A grid that shows which cells have no rule, so where a person's judgment has no rule to lean on.
Model-based user interfaces
W3C task models, abstract UI, UIML 4.0, ConcurTaskTrees		 The nearest ancestors. ConcurTaskTrees is a notation for task models; the W3C working group's notes define task models and abstract interface models independent of platform; UIML 4.0 is a vendor-neutral meta-language for a user interface. The working group closed in 2014, and its notes are off the standards track. Describing the interface above the widget, in a form tools exchange. In what we read, none of these states a law of rights as data, refuses a declaration by name, or ships a suite that any implementation can run. StzZui also closes the channel axis to ten, with what each can carry.

Every cell on the right is written from the page in the left cell, paraphrased, not quoted. A cell the page did not support is worded as what the page does not describe.

Read and left out

Left out on purpose. ISO/IEC 24744 sometimes appears in lists of model-based approaches. Its published scope is a metamodel for defining software-engineering development methodologies, not user interfaces, so it has no row. The standard's page at ISO.

Not read. The inside of Material's and Apple's guidelines was not read in full, since their pages build themselves in the browser; the rows describe what the sites say about themselves in their overviews and documentation search results, and say so. A row that over-reads one of them should be corrected from the source.

What this page does not decide. It does not say StzZui is better. It says where each practice stops, as each describes itself, and where a declaration, a law as data and a suite add a different kind of thing. Whether that is useful to a team is for the team, and the evidence for it is not a study.
