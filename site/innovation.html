Approach · for a decision maker weighing what is actually new

Five claims, each with the instrument that shows it and the limit that bounds it.

Whatever innovation there is here is structural, and its only evidence is this repository. There is no study, no user, and no application of anyone else's run end to end. Each claim below says where to see it, and what stops it being more than it is.

How to read this page

A claim of novelty is cheap and a claim with an instrument is not. Each of the five has a command, a file or a page a reader can run or open, and a limit written beside it. They are claims about structure: how the pieces are arranged, and what a tool can therefore be made to do. They are not claims that interfaces built this way are better for the people who use them; nothing here has measured that, and the practices that do, usability testing above all, are not replaced (Comparison).

1. The unit of declaration is what a person is doing

The claim. A surface is declared by its intent, in a closed vocabulary of 22 verbs and 10 kinds of declaration, with no widget in it. A widget is a consequence of an intent and never a primitive.

The instrument. The grammar and the verifier, and a suite of 73 fixtures with known verdicts. The Language page walks it; What it refuses runs it.

Limit: the vocabulary was closed by reasoning about interfaces and has been exercised on this project's own fixtures and sites. No team outside the project has declared a product of theirs in it.

2. The law is data, and every finding cites it

The claim. The rules live in one versioned file that the Reader, the verifier and any linter load, and every refusal carries the rule, article and right it rests on, so a review is an audit and not an argument.

The instrument. The contract's bindings: of 37 diagnostics, 17 cite at least one rule, 8 cite an article and no rule, and 12 cite nothing. The rules page shows one entry as the tools load it.

Limit: twelve diagnostics cite no rule, and eight only an article, so the grounding is incomplete, and said to be. Of the 123 rules, 80 still need a person's judgment.

3. Refusal comes before anything is rendered

The claim. A flow with no exit, an act with no undo, a promise a channel cannot carry, are refused by name from the declaration alone, before there is a screen to inspect.

The instrument. The verifier and its suite: 46 of the 73 fixtures are refusals, and 22 are lawful twins that differ by one change, so that a tool which refuses everything fails. Run it: node tools/zui/conformance.js.

Limit: only what a declaration can decide. A painted layout is the audit's to measure, so the suite holds 0 fixtures at the level of a rendered surface, by design. Whether a person finds the result usable is not decided here.

4. One declaration across channels, with the gap drawn

The claim. The same declaration is projected to 10 channels, a verb a channel cannot carry is refused, and the places where the law has nothing to say are drawn empty and counted.

The instrument. The grid: 35 of 55 live cells hold no rule. And the cross-channel check on the Channels page, which reports one pair proved and one not proved.

Limit: nobody has perceived any channel for this grid, and the placement of rules on it moves with who reads it (two blind readers agreed on both axes for a fifth to a third of the rules). The renderers that draw, speak or print are not part of this repository.

5. The language is itself declared, and two evaluators are held to each other

The claim. StzZui is written a second time as a declaration in Haro; a court is forged from it; and where the forged court and the hand-written verifier differ, each difference is named and a ratchet fails on any other.

The instrument. Declared in Haro: they differ on 18 of the 73 fixtures, each with its seam, in 8 seams.

Limit: the forged court is not one of this repository's gates, because its judge needs a separate checkout; and it says clean to 10 fixtures that the verifier refuses or warns on. It shows what the meta-language can carry and counts what it cannot; it replaces nothing.

What is not shown

  • No study. Nothing here measures whether a person does better with an interface made this way.
  • No user. No person has perceived any channel for the grid, and the pages have been looked at only by the project's own people.
  • No foreign application run end to end. The one real application planned as a case belongs to another project and has not been run; the screenshot audit has never been run on a real application. The status board lists what exists, what is designed and what is held.

The claims are structural and their evidence is the repository: files a reader can open, and commands a reader can run. If one of them does not hold when run, that is a finding, and this page should be corrected.
