Approach · for a decision maker weighing what is actually new

Zane makes five claims about what it adds, and each claim comes with the instrument that shows it.

A claim on this page is a structural statement about what Zane adds, given with an instrument, so that a reader can test the claim rather than accept it. Each claim lets a reader run a command, or open a file or a page, to check it. The claims are structural, and their evidence is this repository. Each claim below says where a reader can see it at its true size.

How each claim is stated, and what the claims do not say

A claim of novelty costs nothing to make, and a claim with an instrument can be tested. Each of the five claims has a command, a file or a page that a reader can run or open, and a limit written beside it.

The claims concern structure: how the pieces are arranged, and what a tool can therefore be made to do. How users fare with an interface built this way is measured by the practices that measure it, usability testing above all, which Zane works beside (Comparison).

The claim that the unit of declaration is what a user is doing

The claim. A surface is declared by its intent, in a closed vocabulary of 22 verbs and 10 kinds of declaration, with no widget in it. A widget is a consequence of an intent and never a primitive.

The instrument. The instrument is the grammar and the verifier, with a suite of 77 fixtures whose verdicts are known. The Language page walks through the grammar, and What it refuses runs the verifier.

The vocabulary is closed by reasoning about interfaces, and every fixture of the suite and this project's own sites are declared in it.

The claim that the law is data and that every finding cites it

The claim. The rules live in one versioned file that the Reader, the verifier and any linter load. Every refusal carries the rule, the article and the right it rests on, so a review is an audit and not an argument.

The instrument. The instrument is the contract's bindings: of 40 diagnostics, 20 cite at least one rule, 8 cite an article and no rule, and 12 cite nothing. The rules page shows one entry as the tools load it.

Of the 40 diagnostics, 20 cite a rule and 8 cite an article; the others enforce the grammar, where a citation would claim a rule that does not exist. Of the 123 rules, 80 are applied by a person's judgment.

The claim that refusal comes before anything is rendered

The claim. The verifier refuses by name a flow with no exit, an act with no undo and a promise that a channel cannot carry, from the declaration alone, before there is a screen to inspect.

The instrument. The instrument is the verifier and its suite: 49 of the 77 fixtures are refusals, and 23 are lawful twins that differ from a refusal by one change, so that a tool which refuses everything fails. A reader runs the suite with zane conformance.

The claim covers what a declaration decides, and the UI audit measures the painted layout, so the suite holds 0 fixtures at the level of a rendered surface, by design.

The claim that one declaration crosses channels, with the gap drawn

The claim. The same declaration is projected to 10 channels, a verb that a channel cannot carry is refused, and the places where the law has nothing to say are drawn empty and counted.

The instrument. The first instrument is the grid, on which 35 of 55 live cells hold no rule. The second instrument is the cross-channel check on the Channels page, which proves the claim for screen against sound.

The placement of a rule on the grid is a reading, and the coverage document records how two blind readers placed the rules.

The claim that the language is itself declared, with two evaluators held to each other

The claim. Zane is written a second time as a declaration in Haro, and a court is forged from that declaration. Where the forged court and the hand-written verifier differ, each difference is named, and a ratchet fails on any other difference.

The instrument. The instrument is the Declared in Haro page, which shows that the two courts differ on 22 of the 77 fixtures and assigns each difference to one of 9 seams.

The forged court shows what the meta-language carries, and it names each difference from the verifier by its seam. Its judge runs from a separate checkout, beside the verifier.

The evidence for every claim is in the repository

The claims are structural, and their evidence is the repository: files a reader can open and commands a reader can run. A claim that does not hold when it is run is a finding, and this page is then corrected. The status board lists every part of Zane and its state.
