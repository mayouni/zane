Approach · Comparison · for people who already practise UX

Seven established practices judge or build an interface, and this page compares each practice with Zane from its own published page.

A practice is an established way of judging or building an interface, such as heuristic review, WCAG or usability testing, and this page states what each practice decides and when, what Zane takes from it and what Zane adds. The comparison lets a practitioner see which decisions stay with each practice and which ones a Zane declaration adds. Usability testing and heuristic review decide things that nothing in Zane replaces.

The seven practices, with what each decides and what Zane takes and adds

Name, and its pageWhat it decides, and whenWhat Zane takesWhat Zane adds
Ten usability heuristics
Nielsen Norman Group		 Presented as ten broad rules of thumb for interaction design, first developed for heuristic evaluation, where reviewers judge an interface against them. The page names no pass or fail. The ground. Control and freedom, and error prevention, cover the same ground as the law's undo, cancel and exit rules (Rules 79, 26 and 51). Refusal. A declaration that lacks a way back is refused by name before anything is built. The heuristics still judge what only a reviewer can.
WCAG 2.2
W3C Recommendation		 Testable success criteria at three levels, for access by people with disabilities, checked by a mix of automated testing and human evaluation. The idea that a criterion is testable. The audit measures contrast on the painted page, and Rules 62 and 105 set a 4.5:1 floor; the rules do not cite WCAG, and this page does not claim they implement it. Intent and rights beyond access: a flow with no exit, an act with no undo. It is not a conformance claim to WCAG.
Design systems
Material 3, Apple HIG, Fluent 2		 Guidelines, components and tools for building an interface; Apple's guide is organised as foundations, patterns for common tasks, and components. They are applied by people while designing and building. Patterns and principles as the shape of good practice. The same concern stated as a declaration a machine reads before a widget is chosen. The cited pages describe aids such as accessibility checks inside design tools, and no checker that refuses a design for lacking an intent or a right.
Design tokens
Design Tokens Community Group		 A draft JSON format for exchanging named values such as colours and sizes between tools. It says it is not a W3C standard. The habit of making a decision data, so tools share one source. Data for the intent and the rights, not for the look: the law and the media carriage are files a tool loads.
Component libraries and Storybook
Storybook		 Building, showing, testing and documenting components and screens apart from the application. Examples a person can run beside their description, as the lawful twins do. A place to say what a person is doing with a component. The cited page does not describe one.
Accessibility linters
axe-core		 Rules run against rendered pages and HTML for WCAG 2.0 to 2.2. It states that it finds a little over half of WCAG issues automatically and reports the cases it cannot judge as incomplete, for a person to review. The honesty: a case it cannot judge reads incomplete, never passed. The screenshot audit reads not audited the same way. A law behind each finding, cited by number, and checks on the declaration before there is markup. The UI audit measures six rules, a much narrower set than axe-core's.
Usability testing
digital.gov		 Measuring how easily people reach their goals with a service, by established research methods, tracking success and satisfaction. Nothing replaces it. No instrument here perceives for a person. A grid that shows which cells have no rule, so where a person's judgment has no rule to lean on.

Each cell on the right paraphrases the page linked in the left cell, and does not quote it. Where the page does not support a cell, the cell states what the page does not describe.

The parts of each source that were read, and the parts that were not read

Not read. The full guidelines of Material, Apple and the other design systems were not read, because several of their pages build themselves in the browser. The rows describe what the sites say about themselves in their overviews and documentation search results, and the rows say so. WCAG is long, and it was read through its abstract and status sections.

A row that over-reads one of these sources is corrected from the source.
