Language · for people who build languages and the tools around them

The language is also written as data, and a court is made from it.

StzZui exists twice: as a hand-written verifier, and as a declaration in Haro, a language for declaring languages. A court forged from the declaration judges the same 73 fixtures. They agree on 55; on the other 18 each difference is named, and a ratchet fails if one is added or hidden.

The same offence, declared

The verifier refused a wizard that cannot be left, citing two rules, an article and a right. In the declaration the same offence is one constraint: where it applies, the condition that makes it an offence, the sentence, the stable code, and what it cites. It is written from the contract, and the court speaks that code.

DEFINE CONSTRAINT trapped_flow_wizard AS (
  ON wizard, WHEN "not always_reachable contains exit",
  MESSAGE "{name} is multi-step and EXIT is not always reachable: a trapped flow is unconstitutional",
  CODE "TRAPPED_FLOW",
  CITES ["rule:51", "rule:53", "article:intent", "right:leave"]
) RATIONALE "Rules 51, 53; level 2"

The 10 kinds, the 22 verbs and six closed vocabularies are declared the same way, from the pack's own data. The declaration does not replace the verifier. It declares what the meta-language can carry, and counts what it cannot.

Two courts, one suite

How the ratchet works. Every fixture where the two differ is one row in haro/twin-expected.csv, with the seam that explains it and the class of difference. The judge fails if a row is missing, if a difference is not listed, or if the grammar file differs from what the forge writes now. Fixing a seam shrinks the file; nothing can grow it silently.

Where the two differ, in three classes

ClassFilesWhat it meansWhy, by seam
Another code6Both refuse. The forged court speaks a different code, for instance a parse error where the verifier says a field is given twice.S8, S11: the meta-language has its own codes for what is structurally malformed.
Not refused10The verifier refuses or warns; the forged court says clean.S1: a warning has no seat. S3: a check that needs the host world. S7, S14: two checks the condition language cannot yet express.
Court only2A lawful file the verifier accepts and the forged court refuses.S9: a foreign pillar's declaration. S13: a field the verifier reads on any kind.

The ten that the forged court does not refuse are the ones to read first: that class is where a court made from data would say clean to something the law forbids. Four of them are warnings the verifier raises and the declaration does not declare.

Where a difference is a ruling rather than a defect, the declaration says so. The verifier keeps its warning for a named widget until Haro has a seat for severity; it ignores another pillar's declarations where the forged court refuses them; and a declaration still needs no mandatory rationale in a .zui file. These were ruled by the author, and the page does not reopen them.

A grammar a generator can be held to

The same declaration yields haro/zui.gbnf, 4043 bytes of GBNF, the notation grammar-constrained decoders read. It fixes the shape of a declaration: the 10 kinds, the clauses each kind may carry, and the lexical form. It does not fix the verbs or the values, and it cannot refuse an offence such as the wizard above, which is well formed and wrong. The court does that, so a generator held to the grammar still has to pass the verifier. The Generate page shows the two together.

Nothing here states what Haro is for. That is the charter of the Haro project, and this page claims only what its own repository can run: the declaration, the judge, the ratchet and the grammar.
