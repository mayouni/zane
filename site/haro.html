Language · for people who build languages and the tools around them

Zane is also declared as data in Haro, and a court forged from that declaration judges the same suite as the verifier.

The Haro declaration is a second form of Zane, written in Haro, a language for declaring languages, and a court and a decoder grammar are forged from it. The declaration lets a person compare two independent courts on the same files, so that every place where they disagree is named and counted. The forged court judges the same 73 fixtures as the hand-written verifier. The two courts agree on 55 fixtures, each of the other 18 differences is named, and a ratchet fails if a difference is added or hidden.

The trapped-flow offence written as one constraint in Haro

On the refusal page, the verifier refuses a wizard that cannot be left, and cites two rules, an article and a right. The Haro declaration writes the same offence as one constraint, which states where the constraint applies, the condition that makes it an offence, the sentence, the stable code, and what it cites. The constraint is written from the contract, and the forged court reports the same code.

DEFINE CONSTRAINT trapped_flow_wizard AS (
  ON wizard, WHEN "not always_reachable contains exit",
  MESSAGE "{name} is multi-step and EXIT is not always reachable: a trapped flow is unconstitutional",
  CODE "TRAPPED_FLOW",
  CITES ["rule:51", "rule:53", "article:intent", "right:leave"]
) RATIONALE "Rules 51, 53; level 2"

The 10 kinds, the 22 verbs and six closed vocabularies are declared the same way, from the pack's own data. The declaration does not replace the verifier. It declares what the meta-language can carry, and it counts what the meta-language cannot carry.

The verifier and the forged court, judging the same suite

How the ratchet works. Every fixture on which the two courts differ is one row in haro/twin-expected.csv, with the seam that explains it and the class of difference. The judge fails if a row is missing, if a difference is not listed, or if the grammar file differs from what the forge writes now. Fixing a seam shrinks the file, and nothing can grow the file silently.

The three classes of difference between the two courts

ClassFilesWhat it meansWhy, by seam
Another code6Both refuse. The forged court speaks a different code, for instance a parse error where the verifier says a field is given twice.S8, S11: the meta-language has its own codes for what is structurally malformed.
Not refused10The verifier refuses or warns; the forged court says clean.S1: a warning has no seat. S3: a check that needs the host world. S7, S14: two checks the condition language cannot yet express.
Court only2A lawful file the verifier accepts and the forged court refuses.S9: a foreign pillar's declaration. S13: a field the verifier reads on any kind.

The ten files that the forged court does not refuse matter most, because in that class a court made from data would say clean to something the law forbids. Four of them are warnings that the verifier raises and that the declaration does not declare.

Where a difference is a ruling rather than a defect, the declaration says so. The verifier keeps its warning for a named widget until Haro has a seat for severity. The verifier ignores another pillar's declarations, which the forged court refuses. A declaration in a .zane file still needs no mandatory rationale. The author ruled on these three differences, and this page does not reopen them.

The decoder grammar that holds a generator to the shape of a declaration

The same declaration yields haro/zane.gbnf, 4046 bytes of GBNF, which is the notation that grammar-constrained decoders read. The grammar fixes the shape of a declaration: the 10 kinds, the clauses each kind may carry, and the lexical form.

The grammar does not fix the verbs or the values, and it cannot refuse an offence such as the wizard above, which is well formed and wrong. The court refuses such an offence, so a generator held to the grammar still has to pass the verifier. The Generate page shows the grammar and the verifier together.

This page does not state what Haro is for, because that is the charter of the Haro project. This page claims only what its own repository can run: the declaration, the judge, the ratchet and the grammar.
