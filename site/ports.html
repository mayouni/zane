Use · for people who implement Zane in another language

You do not adopt a library: you implement the language and run the fixtures.

A verifier in any language is a peer of the one in this repository. It reads the same pinned files, parses the same grammar, and reports the same diagnostics on the same fixtures, or the suite says which one disagreed. Here is the recipe, and the limits of what a pass lets you say.

The recipe

  1. Vendor the pack and pin its hash. A version number says which edition you meant; the hash says the bytes you hold are that edition. tools/zane/pack.js --check
  2. Implement the grammar from doc/language/GRAMMAR.md: the ten kinds, the closed enumerations and the 22 verbs.
  3. Run the 73 fixtures at the level you claim. Compare by diagnostic id only, never by line number, wording or order. A fixture above your level is skipped and recorded, never failed.
  4. Emit the refusal envelope (the six fields of What it refuses) so one CI step can read any court.
  5. List your differences from the reference, the way the twin ratchet does: each a named row, and a failure on any other. Declared in Haro shows one.

What you may claim, and may not

May: "conforms to Zane v3.14 at level 2, verified against manifest <hash>", your own diagnostic codes in your own words, and your own language for the prose.

May not: extend a closed set. The verbs, the channels, the semantic values, the enumerations and the ten kinds of declaration are closed; adding to one is a language change or a constitutional amendment, and an implementation that adds a verb is a different language that fails the fixtures.

Three claims are separate: conformance (the fixtures agree), envelope conformance (your findings travel in the family's shape), and cross-channel. The last one does not fully pass today, in this repository either: Channels shows its real output, proved for one pair and not proved for another.

Who has

The reference is the JavaScript verifier in this repository. Zing, a separate framework in the Softanza family, is described in its own pinning record as the first conforming consumer of the law, and pins law v3.11. This page states no conformance level for it: that is its own claim to make, and this site has not re-run its checker. A verifier in Zig, with a browser build, is designed. Its first stage, the check, is built: it prints what the JavaScript prints, byte for byte, on every fixture and on a generated corpus (tools/zane/parity.js). It has run on Windows only, and the browser build is not built.

Limit: no implementation outside this family has been written. The recipe is a procedure for a stranger, not a record of one succeeding.
