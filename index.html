The law of user interfaces

Most UI frameworks begin with a physical assumption — screens, widgets, layouts — and try to infer what the user intends. Zui inverts the order: it starts from human intent and treats rendering as a consequence. A human does not come to an interface to click a button; they come to discover, focus, read, compare, select, act, confirm, and understand consequences. Once intent is explicit, interface correctness becomes compilable — violations are detectable before the interface ships, not after users complain.

The decay

Open a mainstream business application and count the offenses: small faint text, verbless buttons, controls floating over the reading, a breadcrumb that only looks backwards, a scrollbar beside empty space, twenty sections where twelve would hold, a menu contradicting the tree it claims to describe. None of this is an accident of taste. These are inherited conventions — copied for decades because they are everywhere, kept because nobody is required to refuse them.

Then came the generators, and the problem found its amplifier. An AI trained on that corpus reproduces its every inherited assumption at machine speed — politely, confidently, and under explicit instruction to be careful. The evidence in this repository is recorded, not rhetorical: every rule numbered above 104 in this constitution was canonized from a defect an AI produced while building a real application.

Meanwhile the discipline that once policed these things has gone quiet, riding the "AI will replace us" wave instead of restating first principles. Someone has to write the principles down in a form a machine can refuse to violate. That is what this site holds.

The story

This law was not written in a seminar room. It was extracted, defect by defect, from a year of building real applications with AI assistance — every correction recorded at the moment it was made.

ZinBank (April 2026) — a full agentic banking platform, built and corrected screen by screen. It yielded the colour laws (one column carries the colour; two families of colour), the widget-honesty law (a card is not a button), the navigation law (the second level turns), and the first law of assistant surfaces (the assistant never masks the work) — Rules 117–121.

Zin i18n (May 2026) — languages added by forking templates left French screens full of untranslated jargon, until language became data: Rule 122. The Refine rebuild (June 2026) — a product site judged unreadable by its own author became the reading-arc law: Rule 123.

The StzWeb site (August 2026) — Rules 105–116, each canonized live from a defect the generator produced while building the site that documents the law. The audit tool caught its own depth-calculation defect on its first run here; that discipline is the point.

The full evidence trail is in the harvest record. Amendment requires exactly this: real usage, operator feedback, documented failure — never fashion (Article VII).

The answer, in three layers

An intent grammar — 24 closed, medium-agnostic verbs (DISCOVER … EXIT). Widgets are consequences of intent, never primitives; no projection may invent a verb, and a SELECT that mutates is a structural error.

The constitution — one JSON file, the single source of truth: 117 rules under 7 articles, each carrying its why, its derivation, its enforcement tier, and the real-world failure that earned it a number. Linters load it, findings cite rule numbers, retired numbers are never reused.

Rendering projections — business web, CLI, voice, game worlds, and the AI-agent surface itself. A projection decides how to render; it has zero freedom over what: it cannot drop an UNDO, hide an available action, or strip EXIT from a flow.

Enter

Read the law — the whole constitution, interactive: search, keyboard navigation, enforcement badges, operator rights, forbidden patterns.

Audit a UI — measure six of the rules on any application UI you can serve same-origin: computed text size, true composited contrast, the full navigation path, sideways scrolling with room to spare, menu/tree coherence. Nothing leaves your browser.

Study the architecture — the three layers and the consumer contract. Follow the roadmap — next up: the Gotchas, one playable page per rule family where you trigger each offense yourself, then feel the law fix it.

Who consumes it

A project consumes the constitution by pinning a version of the law, exactly as a runtime pins a conformance-fixtures file. StzWeb is the first conforming consumer: its linter cites rule numbers, its site scores zero violations under the audit. The Zin platform's Zui pillar shares the same articles and already compiles the intent grammar. Any project can join: the law is one JSON file with a stable schema — load it, check against it, cite it.

The stance is opinionated, and openly so: business applications first. Operators, not explorers — people who use the same screens eight hours a day under pressure, responsibility, and fatigue. Calm over power. Intent over mechanics. Visible over hidden. Consumer-site delight is out of jurisdiction; operator dignity is the whole of it.
