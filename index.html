The compilable language for user interfaces

Zui is a declarative language for intent-driven UIs. You declare what the human is trying to do — discover, select, confirm, undo — and the compiler refuses what its constitution forbids, before anything is rendered. Behind the language: a machine-verifiable law of 118 rules as data. In front of it: projections to any medium — web, CLI, voice, game worlds, AI-agent surfaces. It deploys inside any software project and holds at every phase: design, development, runtime.

Ninety seconds of Zui

A payment approval, declared before a single pixel exists. Note what is absent: no widgets, no layout, no framework.

// payments.zui — design phase, day one
DEFINE INTENT_FLOW approve_payment (
  SATISFIES [SELECT, CONFIRM, ACT]      // interest → review → commitment
)
$ node tools/zui/cli.js check payments.zui
payments.zui:2  error  MISSING_UNDO  approve_payment
    An act declares no way back: ALWAYS_REACHABLE lacks UNDO.
    Rule 79 · The Undo Covenant · Article IV
payments.zui:2  error  MISSING_CANCEL  approve_payment
    A flow that acts cannot be abandoned: ALWAYS_REACHABLE lacks CANCEL.
    Rule 26 · The Back Button is Holy · Article IV

2 error(s), 0 warning(s) across 1 file(s) at level 2

The interface you were about to build has just been refused — at design time, by structure, citing the law by number and title. Declare the way back and it stands:

DEFINE INTENT_FLOW approve_payment (
  SATISFIES [SELECT, CONFIRM, ACT],
  ALWAYS_REACHABLE [UNDO, CANCEL]
)
$ node tools/zui/cli.js check payments.zui
clean — 1 file(s), 0 violations at level 2 against Zui v3.8.0

Status, plainly: that is real output, copied from a real run — the verifier ships in this repository, passes all 30 fixtures of the conformance suite, and needs no toolchain beyond Node. It reads its diagnostic contract and its citations from the law itself rather than hardcoding them. What it does not yet do: the four cross-pillar checks need a symbol table you supply, and one violation (LAYOUT_DISPLACEMENT) belongs to the UI audit because it is a fact about a painted page. The status board keeps the whole account honest.

What makes it different

It is a language, not a library. A human never comes to click a button; they come to discover, compare, act, confirm, undo. Zui makes that declarable — ten declaration types over a closed grammar of 22 verbs — and therefore compilable: a selection that executes, a flow with no exit, an act with no undo are structural errors, refused by name before the interface exists.

Its semantics are a law, and the law is data. One versioned JSON file — 118 rules, each with its why, its evidence, its enforcement tier — is the single source of truth. The compiler checks against it, linters load it, findings cite it, and no prose can drift from what the machine enforces. Amendment requires documented evidence, never fashion.

It holds at three moments. At design time the declaration is checked before a pixel exists. In development every build is linted and every finding carries a rule number. At runtime the UI audit measures the painted page — sizes, true contrast, the trail, the room, the menu against the tree. The full pipeline, including the instruments still in design, is documented facet by facet.

Proof, not promises

This site obeys the law it publishes: it audits itself, in both of its vibes, before any page ships — press F2 to switch culture and notice that your rights don't flex (Article VI). The Gotchas let you commit twelve offenses yourself, safely fenced, each beside its lawful twin, and the vibes exhibit renders one shared truth in both cultures at once. The consumers are real: the reference implementation compiles the grammar and refuses the named violations today, and Zing lints its declarations against the pinned law and scores zero violations under the audit.

The stance is opinionated, openly: business applications first — operators, not explorers. Calm over power. Intent over mechanics. Visible over hidden. Undo is dignity.
