A Zane declaration states intent, not widgets

A Zane declaration states what a user, a person or an agent, comes to an interface to do: discover, compare, act and undo. The screen follows from the declaration.

The thinking →

The law refuses a declaration and names the rule

The law refuses a flow with no exit or an act with no undo, and each refusal cites the rule, the article and the right that it rests on.

What it refuses →

One declaration serves every channel

One declaration can serve a screen, a voice, a printed page or a machine reader. The law refuses a declaration when its channel cannot carry a promise that the declaration makes.
