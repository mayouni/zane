What makes it different

It starts from intent, not widgets. A human never comes to click a button; they come to discover, compare, act, confirm, undo. Zui declares the intent and treats rendering as a consequence — which is what makes correctness compilable: a selection that executes, a flow with no exit, an act with no undo are structural errors, refused by name.

The law is data. One versioned JSON file is the single source of truth: the reader renders it, linters load it, findings cite it, and no prose copy can drift from what the machine enforces. Amendment requires documented evidence — never fashion — and retired numbers are never reused.

It is enforced twice. Before rendering, a declaration checker refuses unlawful structure; after rendering, the UI audit measures the painted page — sizes, true contrast, the trail, the room, the menu against the tree. The full architecture shows every facet, including the instruments still in design.

Proof, not promises

This site obeys the law it publishes: it audits itself, in both of its vibes, before any page ships — press F2 to switch culture and notice that your rights don't flex (Article VI). The Gotchas let you commit twelve offenses yourself, safely fenced, each beside its lawful twin. StzWeb, the first conforming consumer, lints its declarations against the law and scores zero violations under the audit; the Zin platform's compiler parses the intent grammar and refuses the named violations today.

The stance is opinionated, openly: business applications first — operators, not explorers. Calm over power. Intent over mechanics. Visible over hidden. Undo is dignity.
